# Bike History: season list (draft)

One machine per 60-second episode, in order. Dates and winners are from general knowledge and have NOT been checked
yet: each episode gets its own `facts.md` with sources before it is built.

| Ep | Year | Machine | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1817 | Draisine (Laufmaschine), Karl Drais | Two wheels in line, steered, pushed with the feet. No pedals. |
| 2 | 1860s | Michaux velocipede, the "boneshaker" | Pedals and cranks on the front wheel; who was first is disputed. James Moore wins at Saint-Cloud, 31 May 1868 (checked, ep02/facts.md). |
| 3 | 1870s | Penny-farthing (high-wheeler) | Direct drive, so a bigger wheel is faster. Thomas Stevens rides one around the world, 1884-86 (checked, ep03/facts.md). |
| 4 | 1903 | First Harley-Davidson | Owner's choice for episode 4. Milwaukee, the backyard shed. |
| 4 | 1885 | Rover Safety Bicycle, John Kemp Starley | Two equal wheels and a chain to the rear: the shape of every bicycle since. |
| 5 | 1885 | Daimler Reitwagen | Daimler and Maybach put a petrol engine in a wooden frame: the first motorcycle. |
| 6 | 1888 | Pneumatic tyre, John Boyd Dunlop | Air in the tyres: comfort and speed (a part episode, shown on a safety bicycle). |
| 7 | 1894 | Hildebrand & Wolfmüller | The first motorcycle sold in series, and the first one called a "Motorrad". |
| 8 | 1901 | Indian Single | Oscar Hedstrom's machine starts the American industry. |
| 9 | 1903 | Harley-Davidson No. 1 | A shed in Milwaukee. |
| 10 | 1907 | Norton twin | Rem Fowler wins the twin-cylinder class at the first Isle of Man TT. |
| 11 | 1923 | BMW R 32 | Boxer twin and shaft drive, a layout BMW still builds. |
| 12 | 1936 | Harley-Davidson EL "Knucklehead" | Overhead valves and the classic American big twin. |
| 13 | 1937 | Triumph Speed Twin | Edward Turner's parallel twin, copied for 30 years. |
| 14 | 1946 | Vespa 98 | A scooter by an aircraft engineer puts post-war Italy on wheels. |
| 15 | 1948 | Vincent Black Shadow | The fastest production motorcycle of its day; Rollie Free's 150 mph Bonneville run was on a Vincent. |
| 16 | 1950 | Norton Manx "Featherbed" | Geoff Duke wins on the frame everyone then copied. |
| 17 | 1958 | Honda Super Cub | The most produced motor vehicle in history. |
| 18 | 1969 | Honda CB750 | Four cylinders, a disc brake, electric start: the first "superbike". |
| 19 | 1972 | Kawasaki Z1 | 900 cc and twin cams answer the CB750. |
| 20 | 1975 | Yamaha YZR500 | Giacomo Agostini wins the 500 cc world title on a two-stroke. |
| 21 | 1980 | BMW R 80 G/S | The first big adventure bike; Hubert Auriol wins Paris-Dakar on it in 1981. |
| 22 | 1981 | Specialized Stumpjumper | The first mass-produced mountain bike. |
| 23 | 1984 | Kawasaki GPZ900R Ninja | Liquid-cooled, 16 valves: the modern sportbike begins. |
| 24 | 1985 | Suzuki GSX-R750 | A race bike with lights. |
| 25 | 1989 | Lotus 108 (story to 1992) | The carbon monocoque Chris Boardman rode to Olympic gold in 1992. |
| 26 | 1994 | Ducati 916 | Massimo Tamburini's design; Carl Fogarty wins World Superbike titles on it. |
| 27 | 1998 | Yamaha YZF-R1 | Litre power in a 600-sized frame. |
| 28 | 1999 | Suzuki Hayabusa | Top-speed king, and the reason for the 299 km/h gentlemen's agreement. |
| 29 | 2002 | Honda RC211V | Valentino Rossi wins the first four-stroke MotoGP title. |
| 30 | today | E-bike and electric motorcycle | The motor returns, silent. Which machine to feature is still open. |
