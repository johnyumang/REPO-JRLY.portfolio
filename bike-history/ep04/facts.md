# Episode 4 facts: the first Harley-Davidson, 1903-1904

Checked on 2026-11-19. H = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson (early history) · M1 =
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson_Model_1 · MU = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson_Museum ·

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Founders | William S. Harley, Arthur Davidson, Walter Davidson, William A. Davidson; Milwaukee; founded 1903 | H |
| Plans | in 1901 Harley drew plans for a small engine for a regular pedal-bicycle frame | H |
| First engine | 116 cc (H) or 167 cc, "about 2 ps" (M1): sources disagree, so no number on screen | H, M1 |
| Why replaced | it could not climb hills without pedal assistance (H); the builders were "dissatisfied with the performance" (M1) | H, M1 |
| Second engine | 405 cc, bore 76 mm, stroke 89 mm, single cylinder, in a loop frame; power not stated | M1, H, W |
| Drive | "a leather drive belt" (on the 1903 prototype) | W |
| Shed | 10 ft x 15 ft, in the Davidson family backyard | H, MU |
| First race | the prototype was working by 8 September 1904; at State Fair Park, Milwaukee, Edward Hildebrand rode it and placed fourth | H |
| Oldest known | "Serial Number One", the oldest known Harley-Davidson in existence, in a glass case at the museum in Milwaukee; no dated papers for a 1903 build | MU, M1 |

Not used (no source here or sources disagree): the paint colour (our black is a guess), the "Silent Gray Fellow" name
and its year, words on the shed door, Walter Davidson as the first rider, the 1908 endurance run, horsepower, whether
the 1904 machine kept its pedals (our model has them, as on the first machine).

## Model (lib/harley1903.py)

Wheelbase 1.30 m, 28 in wheels with 36 spokes, a loop frame around a single-cylinder engine (crankcase, finned
barrel, head, spark plug, carburettor, exhaust and silencer), fuel tank under the top tube, a leather belt from the
engine pulley to a large pulley on the rear wheel, bicycle crank with chain on the other side, sprung saddle.
Sizes are typical of the type, not measured from the museum machine. No logos anywhere (trademarks).
