# Episode 11 facts: BMW R 32, 1923

Checked on 2026-11-20. R = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_R32 · F = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Friz.

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Designer | Max Friz, BMW's design director (first Chief Engineer after BMW went public in 1922); the R32 was "the second major product Friz designed for his company" | R, F |
| Origin | Friz judged the Helios engine so poor he suggested dumping it in "the nearest lake"; a long-term all-new design became the R32 | R |
| When, where | 1923; F: "debuted at the Paris Auto Show" and "caused quite a sensation" | F |
| First | "the first motorcycle badged BMW"; production 1923-1926, 3,090 units | R |
| Part 1: engine | "494 cc side-valve air-cooled flat-twin", bore and stroke 68 mm x 68 mm, 8.5 hp (6.3 kW) at 3200 rpm; recirculating wet-sump oiling | R |
| Part 2: drive | 3-speed gearbox built as one unit with the engine; shaft final drive through a flexible coupling to a pinion and ring gear on the rear hub | R |
| Part 3: frame | tubular steel, twin downtubes under the engine; rear wheel rigidly mounted | R |
| Part 4: wheels and brakes | tyres 26 x 3 front and rear; rear brake a block acting on a "dummy rim"; front brake none in the First Series, a drum later | R |
| Size | wheelbase 1,380 mm (54 in); length 2,100 mm, width 800 mm, height 950 mm; 122 kg (269 lb) wet; fuel 14 litres; top speed 95 km/h (59 mph) | R |
| Rider moment | none found: neither page names a rider or an R32 race or record in 1923-24 | R, F |
| What came next | layout kept for later BMWs; successors named: R42 and R47; shaft drive on all BMW motorcycles until the F650 in 1994 | R |

## Where sources disagree / not used
- Engine size: 494 cc (R) vs 486 cc (F, tagged "better source needed"). Use 494 cc or say "about 490 cc".
- Top speed: 95 km/h (R) vs 95-100 km/h (F).
- Front suspension: infobox "trailing link with twin cantilever spring", text "leaf spring", a citation says "Indian trailing-link fork". Not used.
- Front brake: infobox says none in the First Series; text says front lever moved to a drum brake later. Use "rear brake" only.
- Engine name M2B33 vs M2B15 (designed 1919 for Victoria) unclear. Not used.
- Not found: price, a first rider, the exact month of the debut. F's "few weeks" design time refers to a different (aero) engine.
- WebFetch summaries were used; quotes are as returned.
