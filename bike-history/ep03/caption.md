Bigger wheel = more speed. 🚲

The 1870s. No chain: the pedals turn the front wheel directly, so the bigger the wheel, the further you go with every turn. Riders picked a wheel to fit their legs.

Wire spokes, solid rubber tyres, a hollow steel frame. To get on, you step on a peg above the little rear wheel and climb up. Brake too hard and you fly over the handlebars: a "header".

The name "penny-farthing" came later, from a big British penny and a tiny farthing coin.

April 1884: Thomas Stevens sets off from San Francisco on a 50-inch Columbia. December 1886: he reaches Yokohama, the first person to ride a bicycle around the world, about 13,500 miles.
