# Episode 3 facts: the penny-farthing (high-wheeler), 1870s

Checked on 2026-11-19. P = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny-farthing · S = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Starley ·
T = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Stevens_(cyclist). Only verbatim text from these pages is used.

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wire-spoke tension wheel | Eugène Meyer, French patent No. 86705, 1869 | P |
| Ariel | James Starley (with William Hillman): all-metal, wire-spoked; 42" front, 24" rear. Year given as 1870 in one section and 1871 in another | P, S |
| Name | from the British penny and farthing coins; first print reference 1891, "probably not used until the machines had been almost superseded". At the time: "bicycles", later "ordinary"; also "high-wheeler" | P |
| Why the big wheel | direct drive: "the larger the front wheel, the further (and so faster) it went"; size chosen by leg length; 60" was the largest standard size | P |
| Typical sizes | Pope 1886: 53" front, 18" rear, 36 lb; Humber: 52" and 18", 24 lb | P |
| Construction | wire tension spokes, solid rubber tyres, hollow steel tube frame; "a mounting peg is above the rear wheel"; spoon brake on the fork crown, worked by a handlebar lever | P |
| Danger | "headers": thrown head-first over the front wheel on a sudden stop | P |
| Thomas Stevens | left San Francisco 22 April 1884, reached Boston 4 August 1884; round-the-world start at Liverpool 4 May 1885; bicycle part ended at Yokohama 17 December 1886, "about 13,500 miles" wheeled; rode a black Columbia 50-inch "Standard" by Pope; first to ride around the world | T, P |
| What replaced it | chain drive (Rover safety bicycle, John Kemp Starley, shown 1885) and the pneumatic tyre (Dunlop, 1888); no longer produced by 1893 | S, P |

Note on "1870s": the title year is the decade of the Ariel (1870 or 1871 in the sources).

## Model (lib/pennyfarthing.py)

Front wheel 52 in (1.32 m), rear 0.44 m, 48 and 20 wire spokes, solid rubber tyres, a black hollow steel backbone that
ends above the rear wheel, mounting peg above the rear wheel, spoon brake on the front tyre with a lever at the bar.
