| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maker | John Kemp Starley, Coventry; "In 1885, Starley made history when he produced the Rover Safety Bicycle" | JKS, SB |
| Company | Starley & Sutton Co with William Sutton: started 1877 (JKS) or founded 1878 (RC): sources disagree, not used | JKS, RC |
| Design | "a rear-wheel-drive, chain-driven cycle with two similar-sized wheels" | JKS |
| Steering | the Rover used indirect steering at first; "later, direct steering was adopted" | SB |
| Why safer | the chain drive "allowed for much smaller wheels"; the low centre of mass "greatly diminished the danger of 'taking a header'" | SB |
| Modern | "Starley's 1885 Rover, manufactured in Coventry... is usually described as the first recognizably modern bicycle" | HB |
| Rower | "The words for 'bicycle' in Polish (rower) and Belarusian (rovar) are derived from the name of the company" | RC |

## Model (lib/rover1885.py)

Two 29 in wheels with 36 wire spokes and solid tyres, a curved backbone crossed by a seat tube (a cross frame),
chain drive on the right to a small rear sprocket, sprung saddle, direct steering. It shows the later direct-steering
pattern; the narration says the first Rovers steered through rods. Sizes and paint are our choice.
