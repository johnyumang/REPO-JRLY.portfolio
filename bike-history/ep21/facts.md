# Episode 21 facts: the BMW R 80 G/S, 1980

Checked on 2026-11-20. G = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_R80G/S · A = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Auriol ·
D = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Paris–Dakar_Rally · P = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris–Dakar_Rally.

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Builder, place, years | built by BMW Motorrad in Berlin, Germany, from 1980 to 1987; 21,864 bikes in total | G |
| Developer | engineer Rüdiger Gutsche, who competed in the International Six Days Trial on a modified R75/5 | G |
| What was new | "the first large displacement multisport bike on the market"; introduced the "monolever" rear: a single-sided swingarm combined with the drive shaft | G |
| Engine | 797.5 cc BMW type 247 flat-twin (boxer), 50 hp (37 kW), bore x stroke 84.8 x 70.6 mm; Nikasil cylinders, electronic ignition, lighter flywheel | G |
| Frame | engine "fitted into an R65 frame" | G |
| Drive | 5-speed shaft drive | G |
| Brakes | front single 260 mm disc; rear 200 mm drum | G |
| Suspension | telescopic fork at the front; single spring/shock at the rear (monolever) | G |
| Wheels | "large 21 inch front wheel"; rear size not stated | G |
| Size for a 3D model | wheelbase 1,465 mm (57.7 in); wet weight 186 kg (410 lb); seat height 860 mm | G |
| Rider moment | Hubert Auriol won the 1981 Paris-Dakar motorcycle class (G: on an R 80 G/S prepared by HPN Motorradtechnik; A: on a BMW R80G/S entered by BMW France). The 1981 rally started in Paris on 1 January and finished in Dakar, Senegal on 20 January | G, A, D |
| What came next | Auriol won again in 1983 (G: 870 cc version); Gaston Rahier won in 1984 (R 80 G/S) and 1985 (1,000 cc version); 1986 R 100 GS (980 cc, Paralever drive); 1987 G/S ended, succeeded by the R 65 GS and R 80 GS | G |

## Where sources disagree / not used

- D says only "The motorcycle class was won by Hubert Auriol" and never names BMW; G and A name the BMW R 80 G/S. P calls Auriol "two-time winner" in 1981 but also his 1983 win his "second bikes class victory": inconsistent wording. The caption uses "won the 1981 Paris-Dakar" and the BMW link from G and A.
- Exact month/year of the G/S launch (as a model on sale) was not given beyond "from 1980". Not used: a launch day.
- Fuel capacity: G lists 19.5 L but also a 32-litre optional tank; not used.
- Rear wheel size and tyres: not stated. Not used. Top speed: not stated.
- G/S expansion: one place says "Gelände/Straße", another "G for Gelände"; the video uses just "Gelände (off-road)/Straße (road)" only if needed.
