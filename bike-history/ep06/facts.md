# Episode 6 facts: the Daimler Reitwagen, 1885

Checked on 2026-11-20. R = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimler_Reitwagen · G = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottlieb_Daimler ·
P = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Daimler. Not found (404): the Grandfather Clock engine pages.

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Builders | Gottlieb Daimler and Wilhelm Maybach; workshop in the garden shed behind Daimler's house in Cannstatt near Stuttgart, from 1882 (G: a glass-fronted summer house) | R, G |
| Patents | engine patented 3 April 1885 (DE 34926); the design patented 29 August 1885 (DE 36423) | R |
| Engine | "264 cc air-cooled four-stroke single", bore 58 mm, stroke 100 mm, 0.5 hp (370 W) at 600 rpm, hot-tube ignition; also ran on coal gas | R |
| Frame and wheels | wooden beam frame; "two iron tread wooden wheels and a pair of spring-loaded outrigger wheels" | R, G |
| Weight | 90 kg dry | R |
| Top speed | 11 km/h (R) or 12 km/h (G): not shown | R, G |
| Drive | 1885: single speed, belt drive; 1886: two speeds, belt and pinion to a ring gear | R |
| First trip | November 1885, Cannstatt to Untertürkheim. Rider and date disagree: R says Paul Daimler on 18 November, 5-12 km; P says Paul travelled with his father on 10 November; G says Maybach rode it 3 km. The video says only "November 1885" and that the sources differ | R, P, G |
| Seat fire | "The seat caught fire on that excursion", the hot-tube ignition being directly underneath | R |
| "First motorcycle" | "widely recognized as the first motorcycle" and the first petrol one; steam velocipedes (1867-69) and others came earlier | R |
| Fate | the original burned in the Cannstatt fire of 1903; replicas in museums (Mercedes-Benz Museum, Deutsches Museum and others) | R |

Not used: wheel sizes (none given), the engine's name and orientation (the pages disagree).

## Model (lib/reitwagen1885.py)

Two wooden side beams with cross pieces, wooden wheels with iron tyres (10 and 12 spokes), an upright single-cylinder
engine with flywheel, hot tube and burner under a four-post saddle, the 1885 belt drive to a pulley on the rear wheel,
two spring-mounted side wheels. Sizes and the wheel diameters are our guesses.
