# Episode 16 facts: the Norton Manx and the Featherbed frame, 1950

Checked on 2026-11-20. M = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton_Manx · F = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Featherbed_frame ·
D = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Duke. (Fetched through a summarising tool: wording quoted only where the tool quoted it.)

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Builder | Norton Motors Ltd, "developed to win the Isle of Man TT" under engineer Joe Craig; made at Bracebridge Street (Birmingham not stated in the text I read) | M |
| Name | "Manx" first appeared on the 1947 racing model, a redesigned prewar International | M |
| Engine | "499 cc (30.5 cu in) 2 valve DOHC air cooled single cylinder", also 348 cc; double overhead cam developed in 1937 | M |
| The Featherbed frame | "brothers Rex and Cromie McCandless" offered Norton a new frame in 1949; "designed by Rex McCandless, of Belfast" (1967 Motorcycle News); "all-welded, tubular"; Norton works team to use it | F, M |
| Frame material | "16-gauge Reynolds 531"; "over forty feet" of tubing | F |
| Name origin | Harold Daniell, testing it, said it was like "riding on a featherbed" compared with the "garden gate" plunger frame | F |
| Suspension | telescopic front fork, swingarm rear | M |
| Brakes | drum; twin leading-shoe brakes added in 1948 | M |
| Size | wheelbase 56 in (1,400 mm); 140 kg (310 lb) dry | M |
| Rider moment | Geoff Duke "won the race on the new design" (the 1950 Senior TT) and set a lap record of 93.33 mph; Norton took "first three places in the 1950 Senior and Junior TT's"; D: he "signed to the Norton works team for the 1950 TT" and broke "both lap and race records in the Senior TT" | F, D |
| Duke's titles | Norton world titles: 350 cc 1951 and 1952, 500 cc 1951 ("three World Championships for Norton") | D |
| What came next | 1954: Norton planned a horizontal engine (F Type), never produced, withdrew from GP racing, Craig retired; Duke moved to Gilera in 1953; Isolastic frame replaced it on the Commando in 1967 | M, D, F |

## Where sources disagree / not used

- Frame date: F lead says "offered in 1950", the Origins section says 1949; patent applied 13 October 1949, specification published 9 January 1952; UK launch Blandford Camp, April 1950. The video says "1949-50".
- The Featherbed is credited to Rex McCandless (F quote) and to the McCandless brothers (F, M). The video says "the McCandless brothers of Belfast"; the Belfast origin is only attached to Rex.
- M says the frame came in 1950 on the Manx; F does not date the Manx's frame to 1950. M's production dates: 1946 (infobox) vs 1947 (lead).
- Duke's 1950 Senior TT win: D does not state it in words (only a table entry and the record claim); F states it. Race date and place not on the pages (Isle of Man implied). Not stated: McCandless in D, Duke in M.
- Power (47, 52, 54 bhp) and top speed (130 vs 140 mph) conflict in M: not used. 1953 stroke 86.1 vs 86.0: not used.
- Isolastic "replaced" the Featherbed in 1967, yet Mercury kept it to about 1970 (F).
- F page carries accuracy-dispute tags. Wheel sizes not given.
