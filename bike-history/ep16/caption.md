1950: "like riding on a featherbed". 🪶

Norton built the Manx racer to win the Isle of Man TT: a 499 cc air-cooled single with two overhead camshafts. In 1949-50 the McCandless brothers of Belfast offered Norton a new frame, all-welded tubular steel, with a telescopic fork in front and a swingarm behind.

Test rider Harold Daniell said it felt like riding on a featherbed. The name stuck.

At the 1950 Isle of Man TT, Geoff Duke won the Senior race on the new design and set a lap record of 93.33 mph. Norton took the first three places in the Senior and Junior races.
