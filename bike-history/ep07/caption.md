Air in a tyre: it began with a tricycle. 🛞

In Belfast, John Boyd Dunlop made an air-filled tyre for his young son's tricycle. His first one was fitted to a wooden disc 96 centimetres across and tested at a sports ground in South Belfast. His patent was granted on 7 December 1888.

He was not the first to think of it. Robert William Thomson patented an air tyre in the 1840s, but it was expensive and not a commercial success.

Then came the race. On 18 May 1889 cyclist Willie Hume won all four cycling events at the Queen's College Sports in Belfast on Dunlop tyres, and later won most of his events in Liverpool.
