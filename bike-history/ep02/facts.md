# Episode 2 facts: the velocipede ("boneshaker"), 1860s

Checked on 2026-11-19 from four opened pages. Not opened = not used on screen.
V = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocipede · M = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Michaux ·
L = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Lallement · J = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Moore_(cyclist)

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pedals | cranks and pedals fixed to the front-wheel hub of a draisine-like machine | M, L |
| Who was first | disputed: Pierre Michaux, his son Ernest, or Pierre Lallement (Lallement: pedals in 1862 at Nancy; whether makers followed his 1864 design or Ernest Michaux's "is a matter of dispute") | M, L |
| Lallement's US patent | No. 59,915, granted 20 November 1866 | L |
| Production | Michaux mass-produced the boneshaker "from 1867 to 1869"; partnership with the Olivier brothers 1868, dissolved 1869 | V, M |
| Frame | iron. M: serpentine frame of two cast-iron pieces bolted together; V: "stiff wrought-iron frame" (sources disagree on the kind of iron) | M, V |
| Wheels | wooden, with iron tyres; "only about 1 m" | V, M |
| Weight | disagree: M says 45 kg; V says a lightweight model was "14 kilograms (30 lb) or more". Not used on screen | M, V |
| Saddle | on "a long flat spring" | V |
| Brake | "a metal lever that pressed a wooden pad against the rear wheel" (M's picture caption calls it a spoon brake) | V |
| Name | "boneshaker", a name from the 1860s, for the uncomfortable ride | V, L |
| Race | 31 May 1868, Parc de Saint-Cloud, Paris; to the park's fountain and back, 1,200 m on gravel; won by James Moore. "Widely cited as the first formal cycle race" but contested; his winning time is given two ways (2 min 35 s or 3 min 50 s), so not shown. His machine is inferred to be a Michaux | J |
| Paris–Rouen | 7 November 1869, won by Moore in 10 h 25 min; distance given as 113 km and as about 130 km on the same page. Not used | J |
| End | fell out of favour after the summer of 1869; replaced in 1870 by the high-wheeler | V |

Not used because not found in the opened pages: the 1867 Paris Exposition, production numbers, the Franco-Prussian
War as the cause of the end, the handlebar-twist brake.

## Model (lib/velocipede.py)

Front wheel 0.90 m, rear 0.78 m, wheelbase 1.17 m, crank 0.13 m, 12 and 10 wooden spokes, iron tyres, a dark-green
painted serpentine iron frame, saddle on a leaf spring, a wooden brake pad on an iron lever. The cord from the brake to
the handlebar, the paint colour and the spoke counts are our guesses. Not checked against a museum photo (image hosts
are blocked here).
