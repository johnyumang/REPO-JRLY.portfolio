1937: two cylinders side by side. 🇬🇧

Edward Turner, Triumph's chief designer, launched the Speed Twin in 1937. It is called the first truly successful British parallel twin: a 498 cc overhead-valve engine making 27 horsepower.

It sat in a light rigid frame. The only rear suspension was the sprung saddle, and the front was a girder fork. It had a 4-speed gearbox with chain drive, a 55 inch wheelbase and weighed 166 kg dry.

It cost £75 and became the standard by which other twins were judged. Later, police escort riders used it from 1952 to 1959.
