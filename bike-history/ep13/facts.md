# Episode 13 facts: Triumph Speed Twin, 1937

Checked on 2026-11-20. S = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumph_Speed_Twin · E = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Turner_(motorcycle_designer).

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Maker | Edward Turner, "Triumph's Chief Designer and managing director" (S); E: Sangster made Turner general manager and chief designer after buying Triumph's motorcycle business in 1936 | S, E |
| When | S: launched "at the 1937 National Motorcycle Show"; E: "In July 1937, Turner introduced the 500 cc Speed Twin, selling at £75" | S, E |
| What was new | "a 500 cc OHV vertical twin in a lightweight frame and the first truly successful British parallel twin"; E: "became the standard by which other twins were judged" | S, E |
| Part 1: engine | 498 cc, 360-degree parallel twin, OHV four-stroke; bore and stroke 63 mm x 80 mm; 27 bhp at 6,300 rpm; one-piece iron cylinder; single Amal Type 6 carburettor | S |
| Part 2: frame and rear | rigid frame; "the sprung saddle was the only rear suspension" | S |
| Part 3: fork | girder fork originally; telescopic fork after 1946 | S |
| Part 4: transmission | 4-speed manual, chain drive; transmission and clutch "straight from the Triumph single" | S |
| Size | wheelbase 55 in (140 cm); weight 365 lb (166 kg) dry | S |
| Rider moment | none found. Use: Metropolitan Police Special Escort Group used the Speed Twin from 1952 to 1959 | S |
| Early fix | six cylinder studs proved weak, eight replaced them | S |
| What came next | 1940: factory in Coventry destroyed in a German air raid on 14 November 1940; the 500 engine continued in the Tiger 100 range, ending with the Tiger Daytona in 1973 | S |

## Where sources disagree / not used
- Launch: "1937 National Motorcycle Show" (S, does not say Olympia) vs "July 1937" (E). S infobox gives production as 1938-1940 and 1947-1966, not 1937. Say "launched in 1937".
- Engine: 498 cc (infobox) vs "500 cc" (text).
- E: the Speed Twin "smaller and weighed five pounds less than the 70 pound Tiger 90" yet cost £75 vs £70; a caption dates an engine 1938. Not used.
- Not found: top speed, brakes, tyres, any race or record (Brooklands), Thunderbird and Bonneville details. Val Page appears only as a name. Wheel size unknown: 3D guesses.
