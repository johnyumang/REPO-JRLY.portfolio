1972: the "New York Steak" that bit back. 🥩

Kawasaki Heavy Industries introduced the Z1, also called the 900 Super Four, in 1972. Its project name was "New York Steak".

A 903 cc air-cooled four-cylinder engine with two overhead camshafts makes 82 PS at 8,500 rpm, in a duplex cradle frame with a front disc brake. It weighs 230 kg dry.

The top speed is quoted at 130 to 132 mph, and it is listed as the fastest production motorcycle from 1973 to 1975. A 24-hour record at Daytona and a lap record of 160.288 mph by Yvon Duhamel on a tuned Z1 followed.
