## S2E1 = Ep 9 — Limang Tinapay (Galilee, about AD 30)
- **Certain (the text):** the feeding is in all four Gospels: Mateo 14:13-21, Marcos 6:30-44, Lucas 9:10-17, Juan 6:1-14. Shared: five loaves, two fish, a lonely place, about five thousand men, blessing and breaking, the disciples hand out the food, all eat and are filled, twelve baskets of leftovers.
- **Only one Gospel:** a boy, barley loaves, Philip and Andrew, Passover near, the crowd wanting to make Him king (Juan 6:4-15). "Bukod pa sa mga babae at mga bata" (Mateo 14:21). Near Bethsaida (Lucas 9:10). Groups of hundreds and of fifties, green grass (Marcos 6:39-40); groups of fifty (Lucas 9:14). Sheep without a shepherd (Marcos 6:34). The death of John the Baptist comes right before it (Mateo 14:12-13).
- **Trap 1:** "the one miracle told in all four Gospels" is **wrong as written**: it is the only miracle *besides the Resurrection* found in all four. Say it that way.
- **Trap 2:** "AD 30" is a scholarly estimate, not in the text. Say "mga taong 30" and call it an estimate. Tabgha (where the Church of the Multiplication stands) is a later tradition; Lucas puts it near Bethsaida. Do not name Tabgha as fact.
- **Trap 3:** the row's "sharing feed a crowd" is a reading of the text (a modern naturalistic interpretation), not what the Gospels say. The Gospels tell a miracle. Report the text and do not interpret it.
- Sources: Bible, four passages (read in the 1905 Tagalog text, see `videos/ep09-limang-tinapay/facts.md`); Wikipedia "Feeding the multitude" (read 2026-11-12); Anglican Communion study guide "The Feeding of the Five Thousand" [summary only]; Interesting Literature, "A Summary and Analysis of the Feeding of the Five Thousand" (2021) [summary only].
- **Verdict: keep, fix the wording** ("bukod sa Muling Pagkabuhay") and the "sharing" line.

## S2E2 = Ep 10 — Batang Indio (Quezon City, about 2012)
- **Certain:** the Code of Kalantiaw (said to be from 1433) was invented by José E. Marco (manuscripts from 1913, *Historia Prehispana de Filipinas* 1917). William Henry Scott showed it was a forgery (dissertation defended 16 June 1968, book 1968). The NHI (now NHCP) ruled it had no historical basis (Resolution No. 12, 2004, approved by the President 2005). The 1957 marker at Batan, Aklan was delisted.
- **Trap 1:** the NHI date. Britannica says 2004; another Wikipedia article ("Kalantiaw") says the NHI admitted the hoax in 1998. Use "2004 resolution, approved 2005", which is what the main article cites.
- **Trap 2:** textbooks. Wikipedia says some textbooks still teach it, but there is no source naming a Grade 5 textbook of 2012. In 2012 an old or reused book that still has Kalantiaw is plausible. Present it as Indio's (fictional) book, not as "the DepEd textbook".
- Sources: Wikipedia "Code of Kalantiaw" (read 2026-11-12); Britannica "Code of Kalantiyaw" [summary only]; repo `videos/kodigo-ni-kalantiaw/` (earlier research).
- **Verdict: keep**, with the two traps.

## S2E3 = Ep 11 — Ang Sugo ng Butuan (Kaifeng, 1003–1011)
- **Certain:** Butuan (Puduan) sent missions to the Northern Song court, whose capital was Kaifeng (Bianjing). The *Song Shi* gives a first mission on 17 March 1001. The *Song Huiyao Jigao* gives missions in 1003 and 1011 led by Muslim envoys. In 1007 an envoy under the ruler Kiling (the *Song Shi* has *Qiling*) asked for the same rank as Champa's envoys. In 1011 the envoy Likanhsieh gave Emperor Zhenzong a memorial on a gold tablet.
- **Trap 1:** the dates: the missions start in 1001, not 1003. Use "1001–1011".
- **Trap 2:** whether the 1007 request for equal status was refused or granted: sources disagree. Wikipedia says it was refused; a Song text quoted elsewhere says other requests (flags, armour) were granted. In the script, say only "humingi".
- **Trap 3:** the ruler in 1011 may have been Sri Bata Shaja, not Kiling. Put the medallion on Kiling and the scene in 1007.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Butuan (historical polity)" (read 2026-11-12); UNESCO Silk Road knowledge bank, "Participation of the Philippines in the Nanhai trade, 9th–16th centuries" [summary only]; W. H. Scott, *Prehispanic Source Materials* (1984 ed.), the standard source for the *Song Shi* passage [summary only, cited in both].
- **Verdict: fix this detail** — the scene is Kaifeng, 1007, Kiling's envoy asking for equal rank.

## S2E4 = Ep 12 — Ang Haring Inilibing sa Tsina (Dezhou, 1417)
- **Certain:** Paduka Pahala (Paduka Batara), East King of Sulu, sailed in 1417 with over 340 people and two other Sulu kings to the Yongle Emperor. He was received in **Beijing** for 27 days. On the way home down the Grand Canal he fell ill and died in Dezhou, Shandong. The emperor had a tomb built there, and the tomb still stands. His eldest son Tumahan went home to Sulu. Two sons (Antulu, Wenhala), his widow and attendants stayed. Their descendants, surnamed An and Wen, still live there (about 3,700). In 2017 they marked the 600th anniversary.
- **Trap 1:** the meeting with the emperor was in **Beijing**, not Dezhou. Dezhou is the death and the tomb.
- **Trap 2:** the date of death: 23 October in the *Ming Veritable Records* (Wikipedia), 22 October or 13 September in other accounts. Say "Oktubre 1417 ayon sa talaan" or just "1417".
- Sources: Wikipedia "Paduka Pahala" (read 2026-11-12); Philstar Starweek, "Commemorating 600 years of a royal voyage" (23 Sep 2017) [summary only]; Philippine News Agency, "607th anniv of ancient Sulu king's visit to China commemorated" (2024) [summary only]; FilipiKnow, "Paduka Pahala" [summary only].
- **Verdict: fix this detail** — the meeting is in Beijing, the tomb in Dezhou. The row's story, "Ang Daan Pauwi", fits even better: the king who never got home.

## S2E5 = Ep 13 — Ang Kasulatang Tanso (year 900)
- **Certain:** the Laguna Copperplate Inscription is dated Saka year 822 = (Monday) 21 April 900 on the Julian calendar. It is the oldest known document found in the Philippines. It is an acquittance: the children of Namwaran are cleared of a debt of 1 kati and 8 suwarna of gold (about 865 g). Found in 1987 at Lumban, Laguna, and deciphered by Antoon Postma (published 1991–92). It is written in Old Malay in Kawi script.
- **Trap 1 (wrong in the row):** Jayadewa is the "Leader of **Pailah**", not of Tundun. It is the Chief of Tundun who acts for him. Either make the medallion "Jayadewa ng Pailah", or make it the unnamed "Pamagat ng Tundun" (no known name, so it is weaker).
- **Trap 2:** "Medang (Java)" is **disputed**: Postma left open whether Mdang is a name or a place; some link it to Java's Medang, others say the word is too common to tell. Do not set the scene in Java. Set it in Laguna/Tondo, with "baka Java" in the fairness beat.
- **Trap 3:** where Pailah, Puliran and Binuangan were is disputed (Postma vs the Pila historians: Pailah = Pila, Laguna). Tundun = Tondo is the general consensus.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Laguna Copperplate Inscription" (read 2026-11-12, with the 1992 Postma translation); Postma, A. (1992) "The Laguna Copper-Plate Inscription: Text and Commentary", *Philippine Studies* 40:2 [summary only]; Inquirer, "Isulat mo sa tubig, itaga mo sa bato" [summary only].
- **Verdict: fix this detail** — the medallion is Jayadewa ng Pailah; the scene is Laguna Bay, 21 April 900; Medang goes in the fairness beat as disputed.

## S2E6 = Ep 14 — Mga Luzones (Malacca, 1511)
- **Certain:** Luções (people of Luzon) were in Malacca when Albuquerque took it in 1511. Tomé Pires lived there about 1512–1515 and wrote the *Suma Oriental* (Cortesão edition, 1944). He describes the Luções as mostly heathen, strong and hard-working, governed at home by elders. Regimo de Raja (Regimo Diraja), a Luções merchant and fleet owner, was made temenggung by the Portuguese after 1511 and died in 1513. A Luzon man served with the Portuguese off India as early as 1506 (Gaspar Pereira letter).
- **Trap 1:** Regimo de Raja's career rests on one short Wikipedia stub and the Luzones article. The search found no second open source on his title. **Open Pires/Cortesão (archive.org has the 1944 text) before scripting.**
- **Trap 2:** the row's medallion (Datu Sikatuna) has **no tie to Malacca**: he is Bohol, 1565 (the blood compact with Legazpi). That is fine for M's medallion as fiction, but the real record beside it must be Bohol 1565, not Malacca. The episode has two real stops in one; keep Sikatuna only as a name.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Luzones" and "Regimo Diraja" (read 2026-11-12); Wikipedia "Tomé Pires" [summary only]; Cortesão (ed.), *The Suma Oriental of Tomé Pires* (Hakluyt Society, 1944), archive.org [summary only]; A. Reid, quoted in "Luzones" (secondary).
- **Verdict: keep, with one check** (open Pires for Regimo before scripting). The Sikatuna medallion is fiction only, with his own Bohol 1565 record.

## S2E7 = Ep 15 — Ang Prinsipe ng Luzon (Brunei, 1521)
- **Certain:** in July 1521 (29 July in the Pigafetta-based accounts), off Brunei, the remains of Magellan's fleet seized junks, and Pigafetta writes that one held "the son of the king of Luzon", captain-general of the King of Brunei. Later historians identify him as Ache, the future Rajah Matanda of Maynila. The captain then, **João Lopes Carvalho**, let him go without consulting anyone (for gold, the crew learned later). Carvalho was then deposed; Espinosa became captain-general and Elcano captain of the *Victoria*.
- **Trap 1:** the Ache article tells the Aganduru Moriz version with **Elcano** in command, "off the southeastern tip of Borneo". Pigafetta, the eyewitness, puts it at Brunei under Carvalho. Follow Pigafetta and say there are two versions.
- **Trap 2:** "on his way to marry a cousin" and the ransom amount are later accounts (Aganduru Moriz, Rizal, Dery), not Pigafetta.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Rajah Matanda" (read 2026-11-12); Pigafetta, *Primo viaggio* (1524), in Blair & Robertson vol. 33, Project Gutenberg [summary only]; borneohistory.net, "Brunei and Spain 1521" [summary only]; Philippine Diary Project, "21 June–29 July 1521" [summary only].
- **Verdict: fix this detail** — Brunei, 29 July 1521, Carvalho (not Elcano) in command; Elcano/Aganduru is the second version.

## S2E8 = Ep 16 — Ang Sabwatan (Tondo, 1587–1588)
- **Certain:** the Tondo Conspiracy (Conspiracy of the Maginoos), 1587–1588, was **led by Agustín de Legazpi of Tondo and his cousin Martín Pangan**, not by Magat Salamat. They sought arms and men from the Japanese Christian sea captain Juan Gayo, **in Manila** (no one went to Japan), and help from Brunei, whose sultan was Legazpi's father-in-law. Magat Salamat was the envoy to Brunei. On the way, at Cuyo/Calamianes, he told the plan to Antonio Surabao, who betrayed it to the encomendero Pedro Sarmiento. It was reported to Governor Santiago de Vera on 26 October 1588. Legazpi, Pangan, Magat Salamat and others were executed.
- **Trap 1:** "Japan and Tondo" — the scene is not in Japan. It is Tondo/Tambobong and the Cuyo voyage.
- **Trap 2:** Magat Salamat is the son of Lakandula (commonly stated; check before scripting). The "pass unseen" power is ironic: the plot failed because he spoke. That fits the row's story beat (Indio keeps a secret) very well.
- **Trap 3:** the Legazpi-family/California passage in the Wikipedia article is unsourced and dubious. Do not use it.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Tondo Conspiracy" (read 2026-11-12); Wikiwand / Kiwix mirrors (same text, not independent); Blair & Robertson vol. 7 (1588 trial documents), the primary source [summary only, not opened]. **A second independent dated source is still needed** (open B&R vol. 7 on Gutenberg).
- **Verdict: fix this detail** — the place is Tondo and the voyage to Cuyo, the leader is Legazpi, and Magat Salamat is the envoy whose words gave the plot away.

## S2E9 = Ep 17 — Ang Tinig (Maguindanao, 1630s–1640s)
- **Certain:** Muhammad Dipatuan Kudarat (1581–1671), 7th Sultan of Maguindanao (from about 1616 or 1619). He allied with the Dutch VOC (1619–1621 and later). Corcuera's 1637 campaign took Lamitan; Kudarat escaped. In 1639 the Spanish entered Lanao; Kudarat went to the Maranao datus, and the famous speech ("Look at what the Spaniards have done...") is recorded by a Spanish source. Spain later withdrew from inland Lanao. Treaty with Governor Fajardo, 25 June 1645. He died in 1671 at Simuay.
- **Trap 1:** who recorded the speech, and when. Wikipedia says "a Spanish ambassador". Other accounts say 1638 and cite Fray Luis de Jesús (Recollect history); Combés (*Historia de Mindanao y Joló*, 1667) is often named. Do not say "his own words" without the chain: it is a speech **as reported by a Spaniard**.
- **Trap 2:** Ternate: an alliance and a call to Brunei, Sulu, Ternate and Makassar are in secondary sources only [summary only]. Keep Ternate out of the title unless a source is opened.
- Sources: Wikipedia "Muhammad Kudarat" (read 2026-11-12, tagged "refimprove"); Wikipedia "Sultanate of Maguindanao" [summary only]; Combés (1667) and Luis de Jesús (1681), primary [not opened].
- **Verdict: fix this detail** — Lanao, 1639, the speech to the Maranao (as reported by a Spanish chronicler), with the 1637 Lamitan escape. Drop Ternate.

## S2E10 = Ep 18 — Ang Sariling Dagat (Mactan, 27 April 1521)
- **Certain:** the battle on Mactan beach, early morning of 27 April 1521. Lapulapu refused Humabon's and Magellan's authority. Magellan led about 50 men against, by Pigafetta, about 1,500. The ships' guns could not reach because of the reef. Magellan was wounded with a spear and a kampilan-like sword, overwhelmed and killed in the shallows. Humabon and Zula watched from a distance. Pigafetta is the only detailed eyewitness.
- **Trap 1:** "Lapulapu killed Magellan with his own hand" is **legend**. Pigafetta has him killed by many warriors. Say "ang mga mandirigma ni Lapulapu".
- **Trap 2:** Lapulapu's age, looks, and "first Filipino hero" are later; Pigafetta does not describe him.
- **Trap 3, continuity:** Ep 15 is set *after* Mactan (July 1521), and the finale goes back to April 1521. That is fine for time travel, but the finale cannot be "the last of the twelve in time order".
- Sources: Wikipedia "Battle of Mactan" and "Lapulapu" (read 2026-11-12); Inquirer Opinion, "The Battle of Mactan, according to Pigafetta" [summary only]; Inquirer, "Lapulapu: Hero behind the myth" (2021) [summary only]; Pigafetta (1524), primary.
- **Verdict: keep.**

# Addendum for timeline version 2: the treasures (worker, 2026-11-12)
Same method as above (Wikipedia raw wikitext read in full on 2026-11-12, marked "WP"; anything else is [summary only] or from memory, marked as such).

- **Ep 9, the lamp (ilawan):** fiction. No source is needed, but it must not be tied to the Gospel scene. Clay oil lamps were common household objects in the period. That is general knowledge, not a verified claim, so it is not spoken as history. **Keep.**
- **Ep 11, the gold of Butuan:**
  - **Balangay:** eleven Butuan boats, carbon-dated individually to 689–988 CE, found at several sites in Butuan with trade goods from as far as Persia (WP "Balangay"). They are older than the Song missions, so "the boats of Butuan" fit the 1001–1011 scene.
  - **"Golden Tara" (WP "Agusan image"):**
    - It is a 21-karat gold statuette dated to the 9th–10th century, found in 1917 on the Wawa River near Esperanza, **Agusan del Sur**, not in Butuan city. It is now in the **Field Museum, Chicago**.
    - Trap: it may not be a Tara at all. Proposed identities include a Shaivite goddess and the Buddhist offering goddess Vajralāsyā. Call it "ang tinatawag na Golden Tara" and do not say it was made in Butuan.
  - Second source still to open: Field Museum catalogue or NCCA [not opened].
  - **Keep, with the trap.**
- **Ep 12, Beijing 1417:**
  - **Forbidden City:** built 1406–1420 (WP "Forbidden City"). In 1417 it was **still being built**: say "itinatayo pa", never show it finished.
  - **Treasure fleets:** Zheng He's seven voyages ran 1405–1433 (WP "Ming treasure voyages"); 1417 falls in the fifth. That Paduka Pahala's party saw the fleet is not in the sources: show the fleet only as "the years of the treasure fleets", not as something he met.
  - **Keep.**
- **Ep 13, year 900:**
  - The v2 row no longer says Borobudur; your message does. Borobudur is 8th–9th century, Sailendra dynasty, Central Java (WP "Borobudur"; the Karangtengah inscription of 824 may refer to it).
  - Trap: the link from the copperplate to Java ("Medang") is disputed, so Borobudur can only be "the Java of that age", never "where Medang is". Better left out of the spoken history.
  - **Fix: keep Borobudur out, or label it a comparison only.**
- **Ep 14, Malacca 1511:** Luzonians are named among Malacca's merchant communities. After the Portuguese took the city (cleared from 24 August 1511), the Luzonian community got its own magistrate (WP "Capture of Malacca (1511)", citing Pires and Correia). Pires puts 84 dialects in the city. "Richest port in Asia" is a common phrase, not a sourced fact: say "isa sa pinakamayamang daungan". **Keep.**
- **Ep 16, the Luzon jars:**
  - Certain:
    - "Rusun" jars from Luzon were prized in Japan for the tea ceremony, and Antonio de Morga described them in 1609 (WP "Tapayan").
    - Naya Sukezaemon of Sakai renamed himself "Luzon Sukezaemon" in 1593 or 1594 after a trip to Luzon, and grew rich selling Luzon wares to Hideyoshi and other lords. In 1598 Hideyoshi took his property, and he fled to Cambodia (WP "Luzon Sukezaemon").
  - Traps:
    - "Very little is known about him from original sources"; the legends are many.
    - "Worth fortunes" is popular telling; say "napakamahal".
    - Magat Salamat died in 1588, six years before. His medallion hidden in a 1594 jar is fiction, and the fairness beat must say he was executed in 1588 and never went to Japan.
  - **Keep, verified enough.** A second source (Morga 1609 in Blair & Robertson vol. 16) is still to open.
- **The clove islands:** not in the v2 table. If used, from memory and **unverified**: the *Trinidad* and *Victoria* reached Tidore in November 1521 (Pigafetta). WP "Tidore" did not cover this. **Verify before use.**

# Addendum: "ang kuwento ni Lolo Pepe" (Ep 9 version 3, worker, 2026-11-12)
- **Travels (certain):**
  - Spain: left Manila 1 to 3 May 1882; enrolled at the Universidad Central de Madrid in November 1882.
  - France: Paris, June–August 1883, again later, and 1889–90.
  - Germany: Heidelberg, eye specialisation under Otto Becker, 1887; also Berlin.
  - Return trip to Europe in 1888, by Hong Kong (left 22 February 1888) and Japan (Yokohama and Tokyo, February–April 1888), then the United States (San Francisco 28 April 1888, across to New York, mid-May), then England (London, May 1888 to March 1889).
  - Brussels 1890; Hong Kong, December 1891 to June 1892.
  - Sources: WP "José Rizal" (raw, read 2026-11-12); Philstar Cebu "Rizal sesquicentennial" (2011-07-02) [summary only]; travel chronologies [summary only]. The day-by-day dates are from summaries; the script names only countries.
- **Languages (hedged):**
  - WP repeats "conversant in twenty-two languages" without a source.
  - Piers Kelly (History and Philosophy of the Language Sciences, 2014-04-23) [summary only] calls the figure part of a mythology and the extent debatable; Rizal himself acknowledged his limits.
  - Documented: Tagalog (mother tongue), Spanish (wrote mostly in it), lessons in French, German and English in Madrid, German practised in Heidelberg, and switching languages in his letters.
  - **Say "marami siyang wika" and that 22 is popular, not established.**
- **Morga (certain):**
  - Rizal worked in London from 1888, using the British Museum; WP names Reinhold Rost of the British Museum.
  - He annotated Antonio de Morga's *Sucesos de las Islas Filipinas* (Mexico, 1609) and finished it in Paris in 1890 (WP "Sucesos de las Islas Filipinas"; WP "José Rizal").
  - The printer was Garnier, Paris (several sources [summary only]). One auction listing gives 1889.
  - Blumentritt wrote the introduction [summary only].
- **Anito (certain):** spirits of ancestors and of nature in pre-colonial belief, and also carved figures (taotao) of wood, stone or ivory representing them (WP "Anito", raw). The term is mainly Luzon's; the Visayan counterpart is diwata. Second source: Scott, *Barangay* (1994) [not opened].
- **Missionaries destroying or taking images: NOT verified enough.**
  - Blair & Robertson's editorial summaries of Chirino's *Relación* (1604) say converts gave up their idols and missionaries destroyed them [summary only].
  - Not opened; not used in the script. Open B&R vol. 12 or 13 (Gutenberg 15022 / 15184) before any episode states it.
- **Fiction (labelled):** that the medallions were datus' anito, their fates, and that Rizal travelled to trace them and drew the kasulatan.

**Clue-stop facts (WP, read 2026-11-12):**
- **Tabon Caves** (Quezon, Palawan): jar burials and the Tabon Man, found from 1962; the Manunggul jar.
- **Callao Cave** (Peñablanca, Cagayan): *Homo luzonensis*, found from 2007 and named in 2019; occupation from about 67,000 years ago.
- **Ifugao rice terraces:** UNESCO 1995. Trap: "2,000 years old" is challenged by the Ifugao Archaeological Project, which dates construction to about 400 years ago.
- **Angono–Binangonan petroglyphs** (Rizal, on the shore of Laguna de Bay): 127 figures, probably Late Neolithic or before 2000 BC; the oldest known art in the Philippines; National Cultural Treasure 1973.
- **Kabayan (fire) mummies** (Benguet, Ibaloi): dated anywhere from 1200–1500 CE to 2000 BCE (disputed).
- **Corregidor:** an island at the entrance of Manila Bay. Important since the Spanish period; WWII battles of 1942 and 1945.

## Dezhou: Paduka Pahala's tomb and the 1417 visit (see also the timeline v1 block)
- **Certain (WP "Paduka Pahala"; Philstar Starweek 2017-09-23 [summary only]; PNA 2024 [summary only]):**
  - In 1417 he sailed from Sulu with more than 340 people and two other Sulu kings.
  - He was received by the Yongle Emperor in **Beijing**, where he stayed 27 days.
  - On the way home along the Grand Canal he died at Dezhou; the *Ming Veritable Records* give 23 October.
  - The emperor built his tomb at Dezhou, with an epitaph of 1418. The tomb is a national priority protected site (1988), and its museum is a national second-class museum (2024).
  - His eldest son Tumahan went home; his widow, concubines, two sons (Antulu and Wenhala) and attendants stayed. Their descendants (surnames An and Wen, about 3,700) still live in Dezhou. They visited Sulu in 2005, and the 600th anniversary was marked in 2017.
- **Traps:**
  - The date of death: 23 October, 22 October, or 13 September (the date Dezhou marks).
  - The meeting was in Beijing, not Dezhou.
  - The Forbidden City was still being built in 1417.
- **For the episode:** the find must be outside the tomb (the rule in SERIES.md). Never inside the grave or the museum.

## Sakai: the Luzon jars, 1593–94 (see also the treasures addendum)
- **Record:**
  - Naya Sukezaemon of Sakai renamed himself "Luzon Sukezaemon" in 1593 or 1594 after a trip to Luzon. He grew rich selling Luzon wares ("Rusun" jars) to Toyotomi Hideyoshi and other lords. In 1598 Hideyoshi seized his property and he fled to Cambodia; he is buried at the Daian-ji, Sakai, which has his statue (WP "Luzon Sukezaemon").
  - Rusun jars were prized for the tea ceremony; Antonio de Morga described them in 1609 (WP "Tapayan").
- **Legend, or unsourced:**
  - "Very little is known about him from original sources": WP says so outright.
  - That Sen no Rikyū prized his wares ("it is said").
  - That he was the pirate Tay Fusa of the 1582 Cagayan battles ("some historians have proposed").
  - The fortunes paid for single jars.
  - Say "ayon sa kuwento" for all of these.
- **Magat Salamat:** executed in 1588 and never in Japan; his medallion in a Sakai jar is fiction, labelled.

## London: what the British took from Manila in 1762, and Rizal at the British Museum
- **The sack and the ransom (WP "British occupation of Manila"; WP "Battle of Manila (1762)"; Tracy, *Manila Ransomed*, 1995 [cited]):**
  - The city was taken in October 1762.
  - Archbishop-governor Rojo agreed a ransom of four million pesos to stop the plunder; only about a quarter was ever paid.
  - The galleons *Filipina* (American silver from Acapulco) and *Santísima Trinidad* (China goods) were taken as prizes. The prize money let Admiral Cornish and General Draper retire rich.
- **What went to Britain and where it is now [all summary only, from the 1762 Archive project, Princeton, and philippinestudies.uk]:**
  - Augustinian manuscripts looted by the troops, among them a *Libro de Consultas* of the San Agustín monastery, now in the **British Library**, annotated by Captain Hyde Parker.
  - The **eight copper plates** of Murillo Velarde's 1734 map.
  - The **Roxas map**, in King George III's topographical collection at the British Library.
  - **Seven grammars and dictionaries of Philippine languages** that Alexander Dalrymple acquired in Manila, at King's College London (Foyle Special Collections).
  - Holdings also at SOAS and, the largest share, the Lilly Library, Indiana.
  - An ivory Christ Child taken home by General Draper, and porcelain, jars and ivories said (by family history) to come from the *Santísima Trinidad*, at Melford Hall.
- **Ties to Ep 10:** that episode already covered 1762.
- **Traps:**
  - No source was found for church bells taken to England; do not say it.
  - "In the British Museum" is not supported for the 1762 loot. Say British Library or King's College.
  - The episode's find must lie outside any collection, for example in a crate never opened.
- **Rizal (see the Lolo Pepe block):** London from May 1888 to March 1889. At the British Museum (Reinhold Rost) he worked on Morga's *Sucesos*; the edition came out in Paris in 1890. This is a natural link: in the same city sit the books taken in 1762.

## Acapulco: the first return voyage, 1565, and Rajah Tupas
- **The voyage:**
  - The *San Pedro* with Andrés de Urdaneta found the return route (the tornaviaje) by sailing north into the Kuroshio Current. It sighted California near Santa Catalina Island and reached Acapulco on **8 October 1565**; most of the crew died on the way.
  - Alonso de Arellano, on a more southerly route, arrived earlier.
  - This opened the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade (1565–1815).
  - Sources: WP "Manila galleon"; WP "Andrés de Urdaneta".
  - Trap: "left Cebu on 1 June 1565" is from memory and not checked here.
- **Rajah Tupas:**
  - The last rajah of Sugbu, after Humabon.
  - Legazpi's three ships reached Cebu on 27 April 1565 and attacked.
  - The Treaty of Cebu (Treaty of Sugbu) was signed on **4 June 1565**; some sources give 3 July. Tupas accepted the Spanish king as sovereign and tribute, in return for protection and trade. W. H. Scott calls it "actually the terms of an unconditional surrender".
  - Tupas was **baptized as Felipe on 21 March 1568**, "under duress" according to WP, at about 70, and died in 1568.
  - Sources: WP "Rajah Tupas"; WP "Treaty of Cebu (1565)" [summary only]; Cebuano Studies Center calendar [summary only].
- **Trap:** Tupas never went to Acapulco. His tie to the episode is that the first return voyage sailed from his Cebu in 1565, the year of his treaty. His medallion in Acapulco is fiction, labelled.
