## 1. Rajah Sulayman of Maynila (first choice)

**Certain (two or more sources agree, and the records name him):**
- He was co-ruler of Maynila, the fortified polity south of the Pasig mouth, with his uncle **Rajah Ache, called Rajah Matanda**, when the Spaniards came in 1570–71.
  - Sources: WP: Rajah Sulayman; WP: Maynila (historical polity); WP: Rajah Matanda, citing the anonymous 1572 *Relación* (B&R vol. 3), Scott 1994 and Dery 2001.
- His people called him **Raja Muda / Raja Mura**, "the young raja", meaning heir. Riquel's notarial record of **18 May 1571** has the lords of Manila declaring themselves "Raha Ache el Viejo y Raha Solimane el Mozo, señores y principales del pueblo de Manila".
  - Sources: WP: Lakandula, quoting Scott, *Cracks in the Parchment Curtain*, p. 13 (AGI Patronato leg. 24 no. 24); WP: Rajah Sulayman.
- **24 May 1570, Battle of Manila against Martín de Goiti.** Talks broke down when Sulayman took a hard line: he would not accept Spanish sovereignty. Each side believed the other had attacked first, and Sulayman's men fought. The fortified town burned, and it is unclear who set the fire. Goiti then sailed back to Legazpi.
  - Sources: WP: Battle of Manila (1570); WP: Rajah Matanda; WP: Maynila (historical polity), citing the 1572 *Relación* (B&R vol. 3).
  - Side note: Goiti first **anchored at "Cavite"** in May 1570. That is a link to Cavite, but the sources do not say Kawit. Do not say Kawit.
- **1571.** Legazpi arrived. Sulayman avoided him at first, then the rulers of Maynila and Tondo made terms. **Spanish Manila was set up on 24 June 1571.**
  - Sources: WP: Rajah Matanda; WP: Maynila (historical polity); WP: Battle of Bangkusay.
- **Religion:** he and Ache were the Muslim rulers of Maynila, with ties by marriage and trade to Brunei. Lakandula's Tondo is described as non-Muslim.
  - Scott and the early reports say the Islam of the ruling families was largely by name. They avoided pork and blood, but the wider population kept anito worship.
  - So "Muslim" is correct for Sulayman. "Devout Muslim" or "sultan" is not supported.
  - Sources: WP: Maynila (historical polity), citing Scott 1994; WP: Rajah Sulayman (infobox, religion Islam).
- **1572.** Rajah Matanda died in August 1572, and Legazpi died on 20 August 1572. Before Ache died, Legazpi granted his wish that Sulayman be recognised as paramount ruler of Maynila. The anonymous 1572 *Relación* explains this by native inheritance law.
  - Sources: WP: Rajah Matanda (Anonymous 1572 Relación, B&R 3; Molina 1960); WP: Lakandula.
- **1574.** After Governor Lavezaris took away their privileges and lands, **Sulayman and Lakandula rose in the Navotas area** during the confusion of **Limahong's** attack. This is called the "Manila Revolt of 1574" or "Sulayman Revolt". Juan de Salcedo and Fray Gerónimo Marín negotiated, and they agreed to terms.
  - Sources: WP: Rajah Sulayman, citing Dery 2001 and B&R vol. 7; [summary] mirrors and Scribd/blog pages give the same story. **(check in B&R)**
  - His son **Rahang Bago** ("Raxa el Vago") was killed in November 1574 (WP: Rajah Sulayman, citing Santiago 1990).

**Disputed:**
- **When he died.** No death record is known.
  - Santiago (1990), from the genealogical documents in the National Archives, says he **lived past 1574** and adopted his nephews.
  - Scott (1994) notes that he is **no longer mentioned** in the 1586–88 accounts that involve his family.
  - Wikipedia itself is inconsistent: its infobox says "d. 1590s" and its category says "1575 deaths". **Use: "hindi tiyak kung kailan siya namatay."**
- **Whether he or Ache was really in charge in 1570.** Dery says Ache had already handed authority to Sulayman. Scott says Sulayman became paramount only at Ache's death in 1572 (WP: Rajah Matanda).
- **"Sulayman III", the ancestry from 1258 and "House of Salalila".** These come from Henson's 1955 genealogy, repeated by Majul in 1973, and are not checked against documents. Do not use them.
- **The Pasay legend** of his children Suwaboy and "Dayang-dayang Pasay" comes from local oral legend cited by the Pasay government in the 1950s (WP: Rajah Sulayman, citing Dery). It is legend, so do not use it.

**Legend or common mistakes (the fairness beat can correct one of these):**
- **"Sulayman died at Bangkusay in 1571." This is wrong.**
  - The Macabebe leader who died at Bangkusay is **unnamed in the Spanish records**.
  - "Tarik Sulayman", "Bambalito" and "Bankau/Bangkaw" are names later given to him from 19th-century family genealogies.
  - On **3 June 2016** the NHCP put up a marker in Macabebe to **"Ang Kabataang Pinuno ng Macabebe"** (the Young Leader of Macabebe). The NHCP said it rejected the names Tarik Soliman and Bangkaw for lack of documents. Its chair, Maria Serena Diokno, said historians had been wrong to say Rajah Soliman died at Bangkusay. An NHCP marker in Tondo in 2021 also names "a Macabebe leader".
  - Sulayman is recorded alive in 1572 and 1574, so he cannot be the man who died in 1571.
  - The mistake still appears on Wikipedia: WP: Magat Salamat says "Soliman being killed along with his troops in battle".
  - Sources: WP: Tarik Sulayman; WP: Battle of Bangkusay; WP: Rajah Sulayman; [summary] Inquirer "NHCP corrects error over true hero of Battle of Bangkusay" (2016); [summary] BusinessMirror, 4 June 2016; [summary] Manila Bulletin, 3 June 2021.
  - Dates: the NHCP marker reportedly gives 31 May 1571, while most sources give 3 June 1571 [summary]. **If the date is spoken, say "Hunyo 1571"** or check the marker text.
- **"Sulayman was killed in the 1574 revolt."** This is an American-era claim and comes from the same confusion. It was his son Rahang Bago who died in 1574 (WP: Rajah Sulayman, citing Santiago 1990).
- **"Sulayman was the first martyr for freedom."** That title is given in Pampanga to the Macabebe youth, not to Sulayman (WP: Rajah Sulayman ref. "sunstar", Tantingco 2006).
- **The name "Maynilad"** ("may nilad"). The early records always write "Maynilà" (Ocampo, in WP: Maynila (historical polity)). If the place is named, say "Maynila".
- **The "painted" (tattooed) Visayans remark.** The 1572 *Relación* reports Sulayman saying the Tagalogs were not like the "painted" Visayans and would not be taken advantage of so easily (WP: Maynila (historical polity)). **(check in B&R)**
  - This matters because Indio's tattoos are a series motif. **Do NOT echo or adapt it in the invented line.** It is a real recorded remark, and mixing it into fiction would blur the line between the two.
  - Note too that the Tagalogs did not tattoo, so the diwa does not "give" tattoos in any Tagalog tradition. The tattoo device stays pure series fiction.

**Right title:** **Rajah** (Spanish *Raha/Raxa*). On screen and in captions write **"Rajah Sulayman"** (also spelled Soliman).
- "Raja Muda" (heir) is accurate for 1570–72, but it is not needed.
- **Is "datu" accurate?** In Scott's sense, yes, as a category: the *maginoo* ruling class included the lakan or rajah and the datus under him, and a rajah was a paramount datu (WP: Maynila (historical polity), citing Scott 1994).
- So "labindalawang datu" works as the series' group word. **Never write "Datu Sulayman" as his title.** It demotes him: in Maynila, the datus were the lower chiefs under the rajahs.
- **Never write "Sultan Sulayman".**

**Living sensitivities:**
- **Muslim Filipino heritage.** He is a symbol of pre-colonial Muslim Manila.
  - Example: the book *Raja Sulaiman was No Carabao: Understanding the Muslim Question* (2001), cited in WP: Rajah Sulayman.
  - [summary] *The National* (UAE), 22 July 2022, called him "Manila's Muslim hero".
- **Some Muslim viewers may object to a Muslim's soul in a bronze amulet** (anting-anting). In Islam, amulets and talismans are disputed and often condemned. Framing rules:
  - (a) The diwa **chose** to keep watch. He is never trapped, never owned, never a servant, never a genie who grants wishes on command.
  - (b) **No one worships or prays to him.** The Lolo "talked to" him, and that stays conversation, not prayer.
  - (c) **No Arabic religious phrases or Islamic invocations in his mouth.** Do not make his faith a costume or a joke.
  - (d) **Do not show his face.** No portrait from life exists. Show him as voice and light only: a glow in the sun on the medallion.
  - (e) Never comic, never sarcastic, no slang.
  - It would be wise to have one Muslim Filipino viewer read the episode before release.
- **Manila pride.** There is a monument to him in Malate and a school named after him in Binondo (WP: Rajah Sulayman). Manila holds a yearly "Kagitingan ng Bangkusay" event, which keeps the Bangkusay confusion alive [summary, Manila Bulletin 2023]. Correcting it gently is a service, but keep it respectful toward the Macabebe hero as well. The two are often confused; they are not rivals.
- **Kapampangan pride.** Macabebe insists the Bangkusay leader was a separate person. Never take Macabebe's hero away from them.
- **Descendants.** Santiago traces his line only through a daughter, Doña María Laran, and adopted nephews. The adopted sons included Agustin de Legazpi, who was executed after the 1587–88 conspiracy. No well-known modern family claims him prominently, unlike Lakandula's line.
- **The medallion's sun and triangle are 1898 flag symbols.** Never say or show that Sulayman used them. If needed, the fiction can say the engraving was added later by the family. That is fiction and goes in the label.

## 2. Lakandula of Tondo (alternate)

**Certain:**
- He was the **Lakan** (paramount ruler) of Tondo, north of the Pasig. On **18 May 1571** he introduced himself as "Sibunao Lacandola, principal del pueblo de Tondo". Historians read "Si Bunao": his personal name was **Bunao** (Bunaw), and "Lakandula" is a title.
  - Sources: WP: Lakandula, quoting Riquel via Scott, *Cracks*, p. 13; Scott, Dery and Joaquin agree on Bunao.
- He welcomed Legazpi in 1571 and was later baptised **"Don Carlos Lacandola"** (WP: Lakandula).
- He rose with Sulayman in 1574 and helped defend Manila against Limahong in 1574 (WP: Lakandula; WP: Rajah Sulayman, citing Dery 2001 and B&R vol. 7).
- He died in **1575**: Scott says "three years after" Legazpi and Ache (WP: Lakandula, citing Scott 1994, p. 192).
- In Scott's 1982 account, he controlled Chinese trade by holding a ship's sails and rudder until duties were paid (WP: Lakandula; WP: Maynila).

**Disputed:**
- His religion. Joaquin "guesses" anito rather than Islam, and the article calls Tondo non-Muslim. That is an inference.
- His ancestry ("descendant of Balagtas"), and whether he was Ache's unnamed cousin who ruled Tondo in Ache's youth.

**Legend or mistakes:**
- "**Rajah** Lakandula" is wrong and redundant: it should be Lakan (Joaquin; WP: Lakandula).
- The *Will of Fernando Malang Balagtas* (dated 1539 or 1589) has doubtful provenance (Scott, *Cracks*; WP: Lakandula; WP: Dayang Kalangitan).

**Title:** "Lakandula" or "ang Lakan ng Tondo". "Datu" is fine as a category.

**Sensitivities:**
- **Many prominent living families claim descent** through Santiago's genealogy: former Presidents Diosdado Macapagal and Gloria Macapagal Arroyo, Jovito Salonga and Lea Salonga (WP: Lakandula, citing Santiago 1990). A soul who speaks could be read as political.
- Some see him as the ruler who sided with Spain, so he is a harder fit for an independence-day line.
- Kapampangan pride: the 1758 *Gremio de Lakandulas*; Pampanga volunteers against the British in 1762 (WP: Lakandula), which links to Episode 2.

## 3. Rajah Matanda, Ache (alternate)

**Certain:**
- He was Rajah of Maynila and Sulayman's uncle; Riquel's 1571 record calls him "Raha Ache el Viejo".
- In **1521**, as commander of a Bruneian fleet, he was **captured by the remnant of Magellan's expedition**, later commanded by Elcano, then released for ransom. Sources: the Aganduru Moriz and Gines de Mafra accounts, with Pigafetta saying he was "much feared".
- He welcomed Goiti in 1570, was baptised as he lay ill, and **died in August 1572**.
- Sources: WP: Rajah Matanda (citing the B&R 3 Anonymous 1572 Relación, Scott, Dery and Molina 1960); WP: Maynila.

**Disputed:**
- How much he ruled in 1570 (Dery against Scott).
- The early-life story about his mother and his cousin in Tondo. It rests mainly on Aganduru Moriz, a later chronicler, not an eyewitness.
- Whether his "Luzon" means only the Manila Bay area.

**Legend:** none major. The risk is the reverse: he is barely known, so a line from him teaches less.

**Title:** Rajah. "Rajah Matanda" is a descriptive name ("the old rajah"), not a personal name; his name was **Ache**.

**Sensitivities:** few. Some may read his deathbed baptism as a defeat.

## 4. Lapulapu of Mactan (alternate)

**Certain:**
- He was **one of two chiefs of Mactan** (the other was Zula). Pigafetta's account, earliest manuscripts c. 1524, records him as "Çilapulapu".
- He refused to submit, and Magellan was killed on Mactan on **27 April 1521**.
- Sources: WP: Lapulapu; WP: Battle of Mactan (Pigafetta).

**Weak (very important):**
- **Pigafetta is the only undisputed primary source naming him.**
- Mojares notes that no European who left a record **saw him, heard him speak, or even recorded that he was present in the battle** (WP: Lapulapu).
- His "words" ("unwilling to come and do reverence to one whom he had been commanding") are reported indirectly.

**Legend or mistakes:**
- **Lapulapu as a Tausug Muslim sent by Sulu.** Senator Bong Go said this in April 2021 and President Duterte repeated it. The NHCP said there is no record of his birthplace or parents, and Rappler and Vera Files rated the claim false or unproven. Sources: [summary] Inquirer, Cebu Daily News, Rappler, Vera Files, 2021.
- The "old Lapulapu" portraits are imaginary. The NHCP's 2019 portrait is labelled imaginary and posthumous (WP: Lapulapu).
- **Spelling:** "Lapulapu", no hyphen, per the National Quincentennial Committee in 2019 [summary, ABS-CBN and GMA, 2019]. Note it was the NQC, not the NHCP.

**Title:** datu (correct). Not rajah, not king.

**Sensitivities:**
- **Very strong Cebuano pride.** He is on the seals of the PNP and BFP and has a city named after him.
- The Muslim-origin claim is a live national argument.
- He would also take Season 1's finale away from Manila and Kawit.

## 5. Recommendation: Rajah Sulayman

1. **Theme.** Episode 8 is independence day. Sulayman is the ruler on record who, in May 1570, would not accept Spanish sovereignty over Maynila. Manila became a Spanish city on 24 June 1571, and Kawit on 12 June 1898 is the other end of those 327 years.
   - Episode 5 closes the circle: the Treaty of Paris, six months later, hands over the same islands Spain began taking at Maynila.
2. **A true correction worth teaching.** "Sulayman did not die at Bangkusay" is new to most viewers. It is firmly sourced, with an NHCP marker in 2016 and records of him alive in 1572 and 1574. It fits the series' "commonly mistaken" slot exactly.
3. **His record is strong.** He is named in notarial and eyewitness-era documents (Riquel 1571, the anonymous 1572 *Relación*), not only in later legend. Lapulapu rests on one source.
4. **Fewer living political tie-ins than Lakandula** (no presidential families). The Muslim heritage needs care, and section 1 gives the rules. Handled well, it honours a part of Philippine history the series has not yet shown.
5. **Fit with the family.** The family lives in Quezon City and Kawit, both in the Manila Bay world that Maynila traded across. Goiti's 1570 anchorage was "Cavite", without a claim that it was Kawit.
6. **What is not known helps the fiction honestly.** His death is unrecorded, so "nobody knows where he went" leaves room for a labelled fiction without contradicting any record. Never state as fact that he "went into the medallion".

## 6. The lines

Indio speaks every line as narrator. Suggested tone tag for the datu line: `[whisper]` or `[serious]`, never comic.

### (1) The invented line (fiction; at most 12 Tagalog words)
- **Main (10 words):** **"Mahigit tatlong daang taon akong naghintay sa araw na ito."**
  - Why: from 1571 to 1898 is more than 300 years, so the line is arithmetically true and invents no event. It only implies the fiction of a waiting diwa.
- **Alternate A (10 words):** "Hindi ako bihag ng tanso, apo. Ako ang piniling magbantay."
  - Why: it answers the Lolo's bedtime story ("natutulog sa tanso") and sets the guardian framing in his own voice.
- **Alternate B (10 words):** "Matagal kong binantayan ang look na ito. Ikaw naman ngayon."
  - Why: Kawit and Maynila are both on Manila Bay. It hands the watch to Indio without claiming any real deed.
- **Do not use:** any line saying he "never surrendered" (he made terms in 1571 and 1574), anything about Bangkusay, any Islamic phrase, or any echo of his real recorded words (the "painted" remark, or the refusal recorded in 1570).

### (2) Real-record line for the fairness beat (Tagalog, at most 20 words, no slang)
- **16 words:** **"Totoo si Rajah Sulayman ng Maynila: lumaban noong 1570, nag-alsa noong 1574, hindi namatay sa Bangkusay."**
- Shorter fallback (12 words): "Totoo si Rajah Sulayman: lumaban sa Maynila noong 1570, nag-alsa noong 1574."
- Optional add-on if there is time: "Hindi tiyak kung kailan siya namatay."

### (3) Two-sentence label (caption and pinned comment)
> Kathang-isip ang diwa sa medalyon at ang linyang sinabi niya; walang ganoong salitang naitala mula kay Rajah Sulayman. Totoo siyang rajah ng Maynila na lumaban sa mga Espanyol noong 1570 at nag-alsa noong 1574, at hindi siya ang pinunong namatay sa Bangkusay noong 1571.

(This label sits alongside the standing line "Kathang-isip sina Indio at Usman; totoo ang kasaysayan.")

### (4) Power and limit of the family medallion (fiction)
- **Power, "Ang Pintuan" (the door):** when the holder reads a real historical marker aloud, Sulayman's diwa opens a door to that exact place and day.
  - The heat is him waking; the sun on the bronze glows while the door is open. This makes him the one who has made every jump since Episode 1, and the one who "answered" the Lolo.
  - Thematic link only, never stated as history: Maynila sat at the river mouth, the gateway of the bay's trade.
- **Limit:** he opens doors only to moments a real marker records, and only as a witness. He cannot take anyone to a day no marker names, cannot send them forward in time, and nothing he opens lets the holder change what happened.
  - This fits the series' witness rule and explains why markers matter.
  - Optional corollary, if the director wants it: a marker with an error opens blurred. That would tie in with the lens, and the Bangkusay marker is the real example of a marker being corrected. Keep this optional so it does not clash with the lens's own power.

## 7. Twelve medallions for Season 2: first rough list

Rules used: the person is named in a dated primary record or a reliable chronicle; balanced at four Luzon, four Visayas and four Mindanao; no one whose existence is doubtful. **Sulayman is #1, the family medallion.** If the family medallion is not one of the twelve, use the first reserve.

| # | Name, title | Place | Period | One line on the record | Strength of sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rajah Sulayman** | Maynila (Luzon) | fl. 1570–1575 | Co-ruler of Maynila; fought Goiti in 1570; rose with Lakandula in 1574. | Strong: Riquel 1571, Relación 1572, Scott, Dery, Santiago |
| 2 | **Lakandula (Bunao), Lakan** | Tondo (Luzon) | before 1570 – d. 1575 | Lakan of Tondo; controlled the Chinese trade; baptised Don Carlos; fought Limahong in 1574. | Strong: Riquel 1571, Scott, Santiago |
| 3 | **Rajah Ache (Rajah Matanda)** | Maynila (Luzon) | c. 1521 – Aug 1572 | Bruneian fleet commander captured by Magellan's survivors in 1521; later Rajah of Maynila. | Good: Pigafetta, Mafra, Aganduru Moriz, Relación 1572 |
| 4 | **Magat Salamat, datu** | Tondo (Luzon) | fl. 1571 – executed 1588 | Son of Lakandula; a leader of the 1587–88 Tondo conspiracy against Spain. | Good: conspiracy trial records via Scott, Santiago, Dery. His biography article contains the Sulayman mistake, so use the conspiracy facts only. |
| 5 | **Lapulapu, datu** | Mactan (Visayas) | fl. 1521 | One of two chiefs of Mactan; refused tribute; Magellan died on Mactan, 27 Apr 1521. | Existence certain; details weak (only Pigafetta) |
| 6 | **Rajah Humabon** (baptised Carlos) | Cebu (Visayas) | fl. 1521 | Chief of Cebu; first recorded baptism in the islands, April 1521; Magellan's ally. | Good, but one main source (Pigafetta) |
| 7 | **Rajah Tupas** (baptised Felipe) | Cebu (Visayas) | c. 1497 – 1568 | Ruler of Cebu when Legazpi attacked in April 1565; treaty of 4 June 1565. | Good: Legazpi expedition records |
| 8 | **Datu Sikatuna** | Bohol (Visayas) | fl. 1565 | Blood compact with Legazpi, March 1565. The date is disputed, 16 or 25 March, and NHI Resolution No. 4 (2005) placed it at Loay. | Good: Legazpi expedition records |
| 9 | **Rajah Kiling** | Butuan (Mindanao) | fl. 1001–1009 | Ruler of Butuan who sent envoys to the Song court; the name is a Chinese transliteration. | Good (Song Chinese records), but the name's form is uncertain |
| 10 | **Rajah Siawi (Siagu)** | Butuan and Calaga (Mindanao) | fl. 1521 | Ruler of Butuan with Rajah Kolambu at Limasawa (Mazaua); attended the first Mass, 31 Mar 1521. | Good, but one main source (Pigafetta) |
| 11 | **Sultan Muhammad Dipatuan Kudarat** | Maguindanao (Mindanao) | 1581–1671; sultan c. 1616/19–1671 | Held off Spanish invasions; Corcuera took Lamitan in 1637, but Kudarat escaped to Lanao. | Strong: Jesuit and Spanish records, NHCP, Majul |
| 12 | **Sultan Muwallil Wasit I (Rajah Bongsu)** | Sulu (Mindanao) | fl. c. 1610–1650 | Sultan of Sulu during the Spanish attacks on Jolo in the 1630s. | Good: Nicholl 1991, Majul 1973 (list seen on WP only) |

**Reserves:**
- **Rajah Kolambu** (Limasawa, 1521, Pigafetta).
- **Datu Sigala** (Loboc, Bohol, 1565; blood compact days after Sikatuna's) [summary, Bohol Chronicle].
- **Kapitan Laut Buisan** (Maguindanao, r. 1597–1616; father of Kudarat).
- **Agustin de Legazpi** (Tondo, d. 1589; Sulayman's adopted son).

**Gap to flag:** no pre-colonial ruler from the Cordillera, Ilocos, Cagayan, Pangasinan or Bicol is reliably named in early records with a datable deed. Northern Luzon is under-represented for a real reason, not by choice. If the director wants the north, the honest option is an unnamed figure labelled as such, as the NHCP did for Macabebe. That cannot be a named datu soul under the current rule.

**Sensitivities for the Mindanao four:**
- #11 and #12 are Muslim sultans with living royal houses. Datu families in Maguindanao use the title in politics today.
- The **Sulu succession and the Sabah claim are politically live**. Keep #12 away from any claim talk, or replace him with Buisan.
- All the Islamic-framing rules from section 1 apply. "Sultan" is their correct title, and "datu" is acceptable only as the series' group word.

**Left out on purpose:**
- **Kalantiaw** and his "Code". A 1913 forgery by José E. Marco, exposed by W. H. Scott in his doctoral defence of 16 June 1968 and in *Prehispanic Source Materials*. The NHI/NHCP declared it fraudulent in 2005 (WP: Code of Kalantiaw; WP: Kalantiaw). The series already did a Kalantiaw video; never make him a medallion.
- **Datu Puti, Sumakwel, Marikudo and the Ten Bornean Datus.** Their only source is the *Maragtas* of Pedro Monteclaro (1907), with no surviving older source (WP: Maragtas). Legend.
- **"Tarik Sulayman", Bambalito, Bangkaw.** The Bangkusay leader is real but **unnamed** in the records, and the NHCP rejected these names in 2016. A soul with an invented name would break the rule.
- **Dayang Kalangitan, Gat Lontok, Rajah Salalila, Prince Balagtas, Sasanban.** These rest on the doubtful *Will of Fernando Malang Balagtas* and on Henson's genealogies (WP: Dayang Kalangitan; WP: Lakandula, citing Scott, *Cracks*).
- **Rajah Ahmad and "Avirjirkaya" (the 1258 founding of Maynila).** Only in Henson 1955.
- **Sri Lumay** of Cebu. A folk narrative (the Aginid tradition); the Wikipedia page now redirects to "Cebuano literature".
- **Sharif Kabungsuwan.** Real in the Maguindanao *tarsila* (genealogies), but the dates and details are traditional and the article mixes in legend. Keep him as a "maybe" only after a closer check.
- **The "Chief of Tundun" in the Laguna Copperplate Inscription (900 CE).** Real, but unnamed in the text; Namwaran is a debtor, not a ruler.
