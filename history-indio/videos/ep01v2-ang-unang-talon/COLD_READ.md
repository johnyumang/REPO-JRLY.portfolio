# Cold-reader test — Episode 1, version 2

**The test.** A reader who knew nothing about the series was given only the master script with the comment
lines removed (spoken lines and picture lines), read it once, and was asked to retell it in exactly five
sentences and to list everything that confused them.

**Result: passed.** The retelling was correct and complete, in order:

> 1. Si Indio, isang tambay sa Quezon City, ay hindi na naman natanggap sa trabaho dahil wala siyang cedula, at
>    pati ang lumang medalyon ng Lolo niya ay sinabihang tanso lang sa sanglaan.
> 2. Kinagabihan, sumilong siya sa ulan sa Pugad Lawin Shrine, binasa nang malakas ang marker, uminit ang
>    medalyon, at may itim na asong nagsasalita na lumundag mula sa liwanag.
> 3. Bigla siyang napunta sa Agosto 1896, sa gitna ng daan-daang Katipunero, at dahil wala siyang cedula ay
>    inakala siyang espiya at tinutukan ng itak sa leeg ng batang si Islaw.
> 4. Umamin siya na wala talaga siyang kahit ano, nakisimpatiya si Islaw na wala ring cedula, at nang humiga
>    ang mga aso ng kampo sa paanan niya ay kinalagan siya at nasaksihan niya ang pagpunit ng mga cedula.
> 5. Pagbalik niya sa kasalukuyan ay may umuusok na marka na siya sa braso, may babaeng nakatitig sa kanya sa
>    kabilang kalye, at ang taga-sanglaan pala ay palihim na kumuha ng hulma ng medalyon niya.

Want, fall, comeback, in the reader's words: he wanted a job and was turned away for lacking a cedula, nearly
had his throat cut in 1896 for lacking the same paper, and came back by telling the plain truth that he has
nothing, which won over Islaw, with the camp dogs sealing it.

Questions that would make the reader watch the next one: who is the woman under the lamp post; what does the
pawnshop want with a copy of the medallion (and who was the Lolo). Runner-up: what is Usman.

Best moment: the confession answered by "Wala rin akong cedula. Hindi kayang bayaran ni Itay."; then Islaw
with nothing to tear, raising his fist. Jokes that landed: "Pati medalyon ko, bagsak sa interview."; "Kinausap
ko sila. May bayad nga lang: hapunan mo."; "Uso pa rin pala."

## What confused the reader, and what was changed after the test

| Finding | Change made |
|---|---|
| "Cedula" is not explained until late; a child would not know the word for two minutes | The woman at the window now says "Walang cedula, 'yung resibo ng buwis mo." |
| Why would Katipuneros demand the paper they are about to tear, and why from a boy who has none? | The demand now comes from the adult: someone informed on them this week, so everyone here brought his cedula tonight; a stranger with no paper and no one to vouch for him is a spy. Islaw only asks who brought him |
| "Kasusumbong lang sa amin sa mga Kastila" is a mouthful | "May nagsumbong sa amin sa Kastila nitong linggo." |
| Three unrelated bits after the climax (the name of the place, "pag-aawayan pa nila", the shirt inside out) drain the tearing | The place question and Usman's growl move BEFORE the tearing; the shirt trick is cut from this episode (it will be planted by Episode 12's own friend). The past now ends on the kept line and "Kuya, babalik ka ba?" |
| "Pag-aawayan pa nila 'yan" is unclear unless you know the dispute | "Balang araw, pag-aawayan nila kung saan mismo ito nangyari." |
| The line where Islaw explains the cedula feels like a lesson | Cut from four sentences to two |
| The speaker "SANGLAAN" against the "katulong" in the picture: one person or two? | Two speakers: MAY-ARI (face never shown) and KATULONG (the hand, the wax, the nail) |
| Reading the marker "because he had nothing to do" is thin | "Wala akong kausap. Marker na lang." |
| The return just cuts; what is the mark? | "Kuya, babalik ka ba?"; Usman, fading: "May marka ka na, par. Una pa lang 'yan." |
| "Sanay na ako. Chill lang." fell flat | Replaced: "Pang-anim na 'malas' ko na rin 'yan ngayong buwan." |

Left as it is, on purpose (these are the questions of the series, planted here and paid later): the three
slanted strokes over the pawnshop door; the nail; the face on the tarpaulin; why the dog knows his name; who the woman is; the leader seen
from behind, unnamed in the story (he is named in the trivia after it). The cold open tells that a blade will
be at his neck; that is the hook, and the surprise kept for the scene is how he gets out.

A ten-year-old, per the reader: follows the dog, the blade and the bread. A grandmother: follows the main
story; the wax and the nail at the end go fast, so shots 10 and 38 are held longer (see `shots.md`).
