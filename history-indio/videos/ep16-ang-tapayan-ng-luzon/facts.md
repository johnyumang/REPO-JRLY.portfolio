# Facts — Episode 16 (Season 2 Episode 8): Ang Tapayan ng Luzon — version 1 (written 2026-11-12, to be checked against story version 2 before frames)

**What is true:** Luzon jars ("Rusun") were prized in Japan for the tea ceremony, and Antonio de Morga described them in 1609. Naya Sukezaemon of Sakai renamed himself Luzon Sukezaemon in 1593 or 1594 after a trip to Luzon and grew rich selling Luzon wares to Toyotomi Hideyoshi and other lords. In 1598 Hideyoshi seized his property and he fled to Cambodia; he is buried at the Daian-ji in Sakai, which has his statue. The Tondo Conspiracy of 1587–1588 was led by Agustín de Legazpi of Tondo and his cousin Martín Pangan. They sought help from a Japanese captain in Manila (Juan Gayo) and from the sultan of Brunei. Magat Salamat was the envoy to Brunei; at Cuyo the plan was told to Antonio Surabao, who betrayed it. The leaders, Magat Salamat among them, were executed.
**Witness rule:** the jump goes to Sakai about 1594; the people there (merchants, crews, Sukezaemon) are generic far figures and get no words. Tondo 1587–1588 is seen only through the lens: Legazpi, Pangan and Magat Salamat are far figures from behind, with no words and no recognisable faces.
**Fiction, on purpose:** Indio, M, Usman, Tubal and his men, the medallions and their powers, the medallion hidden in a Sakai jar (Magat Salamat never went to Japan), the kappa's pact, the bow found under the jar, the diwa's line, the fox woman.
**Usman:** inside the jump only (Sakai, 1594); he vanishes when the Sandugo cools. In the present (temple gate, hotel) there is no dog.
**The find:** the medallion of Magat Salamat, "Ang Lihim" (unseen while silent; never to steal or to harm); Ang Busog at Palaso. **Mga Kakampi:** the kappa (repays with labour or a secret). **Knowledge:** 33% (ebook spine).

**Note on the diwa line:** invented under the first approved exception (the soul of a real datu in a medallion). Magat Salamat's real record is stated beside it in the fairness beat: an envoy of the Tondo conspiracy, executed in 1588; the plot was betrayed after it was told at Cuyo. The line echoes that record, it does not add to it.

## Second sources added (ebook worker, 2026-11-12; search summaries only, pages not opened)
- Luzon Sukezaemon (Naya Sukezaemon of Sakai): a Japanese biographical entry dates his presentation of Luzon pots and other goods to Hideyoshi to 4 September 1594; another account says 1593 and fifty tea-leaf jars. Several commentators say the story is legend and even his existence is questioned; "very little is known from original sources". This is why the script frames him as "ayon sa kuwento" and puts his life under "pinagtatalunan, o kuwento lang". Sources: https://japanese-wiki-corpus.github.io/person/Sukezaemon%20RUSON.html ; https://opinion.inquirer.net/102995/the-rusontsubos-from-luzon ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_Sukezaemon ; https://samurai-archives.com/wiki/Luzon_Sukezaemon
- The jars themselves are better documented: a 1594 letter of Hideyoshi asking for jars from Manila, and a 1594 bill of sale of seven Luzon jars in Kyoto by a Tsuruga merchant; the jars were Chinese-made storage jars traded through Luzon (Inquirer, Ambeth Ocampo columns): https://opinion.inquirer.net/102995/the-rusontsubos-from-luzon ; https://opinion.inquirer.net/36656/more-on-the-mysterious-luzon-jars
- Tondo Conspiracy 1587–88: leaders Agustín de Legazpi and Magat Salamat (son of Lakandula), with kin; discovered after Magat Salamat revealed the plan to Antonio Surabao, who reported it (one source dates the discovery 26 October 1588); Legazpi and Salamat executed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondo_Conspiracy ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agustin_de_Legazpi ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magat_Salamat (Blair & Robertson vol. 7 still to be read for a primary source.)
