# Facts — Episode 29 (Season 3 Episode 9): Ang Unang Apat na Lalim (written 2026-11-12 from story version 2, Kabanata 29)

**What is true:** Dante's *Inferno* is a poem (early fourteenth century) whose hell has nine circles; in cantos 21–22 the barrators, who trafficked in public office, are sunk in boiling pitch and guarded by demons with hooks. The Qur'an (15:44) says Jahannam has seven gates; whether they are seven levels is a reading of scholars, not the explicit text. Spanish accounts of early Tagalog belief (Plasencia, 1589) describe an underworld of suffering, the Kasanaan. The Greek *martys* means "witness" and is the root of "martyr".
**Label for captions (Season 3 model):** "Ang tagpo ay ayon sa kuwento ng serye; hinango ang pitong lalim sa Inferno ni Dante, sa Qur'an 15:44 at sa lumang paniniwala ng mga Tagalog sa Kasanaan."
**Fiction, on purpose:** Indio, Usman, Lolo Isidro, Teddy Bracken, Sinag, the seven depths and their princes (Limot, Hilig, Sakim, Kurakot, Poot, Linlang, Palalo), the chain of ash, the bleeding marks, the shadows in the pitch, Alcaín Builders and Tubal. No real person, living or dead, is shown or named in the depths; the shadows have no faces.
**Usman:** present (below Banahaw is not the present day).
**The find:** the father, found and freed; the first bleeding of the marks. **Power used:** Ang Punyal (cuts what binds, wounds nothing). **Knowledge:** 85%.
