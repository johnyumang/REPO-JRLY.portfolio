# Season 3 "Mga Lagusan": source check (worker, 2026-11-12)

Research done before the season's scripts, covering only what the story check cannot change. One section per topic.
Each section says what the source is, what it actually says, what is legend or later invention, and where the script could trip.
Sources: Wikipedia articles were read in full as raw wikitext, fetched 2026-11-12. Anything marked **[summary only]** was seen only as a search summary and must be opened before it goes into a `facts.md`.
Scripture is quoted from **Ang Dating Biblia (1905)**. The text was fetched 2026-11-12 from the `abtag` Tagalog text files of the nehemiaharchives/bbl repository on GitHub, the same text used for Episode 9.
Season 3 rules carried into every section:
- Scripture is quoted exactly and never mocked.
- God is given no invented words.
- Every legend is labelled as legend in the fairness beat.
- No story detail of later episodes goes into this public file.

## 1. Biringan (Samar folklore)
- **Source:** Wikipedia "Biringan" (short article, few printed sources).
- **What the folklore says:**
  - An invisible, "ultramodern" city between Gandara and Pagsanghan, Samar.
  - Peopled by *engkanto* (shapeshifters who can take human form, known by having no philtrum) and their children with humans.
  - Said to hold gold.
  - Said to have **seven portals** scattered across Samar, one at an old tree on the grounds of Northwest Samar State University in Calbayog.
  - Seafarers tell of a "dazzling city of light" seen for a few minutes on moonless nights.
  - Witnesses are said in the stories to have been possessed.
- **Name:** WP gives the Waray meaning as the place "where the lost are looked for" (*hanapan ng mga nawawala*). There is only one source for this; say "ayon sa isang paliwanag".
- **When the stories spread:** from the **1960s** (an American shipment of building materials said to be addressed to "Biringan"). A Japanese mining company is said to have dug near San Jorge and quit after accidents; WP calls that a legend too. Later TV and the internet spread it further. Films: *T2* (2009, Chito S. Roño), *Gusto Kita With All My Hypothalamus* (2018), *Swipe Right to Disappear* (2019).
- **Traps:**
  - Biringan is **folklore only**. There is no map, record or survey of it, and the mining and shipment stories are themselves legends.
  - The "seven portals" are part of the folklore. If the story uses seven gates, label them as the legend's number.
  - Never present a real place or the university as a "real" gate. Say "ayon sa kuwento".
- **Verdict:** usable as a labelled legend.

## 2. The Tower of Babel (Genesis 11) and Babylon's ziggurat
- **The text (Genesis 11:1–9, Ang Dating Biblia):**
  - v1: "At ang buong lupa ay iisa ang wika at iisa ang salita."
  - v4: "…Magtayo tayo ng isang bayan natin at ng isang moog, na ang taluktok niyaon ay aabot hanggang sa langit, at magtaglay tayo ng isang ngalan…"
  - v9: "Kaya ang pangalang itinawag ay Babel; sapagka't doon ginulo ng Panginoon ang wika ng buong lupa…"
  - The plain is **Sinar** (Shinar). The builders use bricks and bitumen ("betun"). The building stops when the people are scattered (v8).
- **Archaeology** (WP "Etemenanki", "Tower of Babel"):
  - The ziggurat of Babylon was **Etemenanki**, dedicated to Marduk and tied to the Esagila temple. Modern scholars most often name it as the inspiration for the story.
  - Its history goes back to Hammurabi's time. Sennacherib destroyed Babylon in **689 BC**; Esarhaddon, Nabopolassar and **Nebuchadnezzar II** rebuilt it.
  - The "Esagila tablet" (a 229 BC copy of an older text, translated by George Smith in 1876) gives the height as about **91 m**, on a square base of 91 m a side. Koldewey's excavations (from 1913) confirmed the mud-brick core.
  - The "Tower of Babel Stele" (Schøyen Collection, found 2003) shows it and dates from 604–562 BC.
  - WP says the authenticity of a Nebuchadnezzar inscription said to come from Koldewey's dig is uncertain. Don't quote it.
- **Traps:**
  - Don't say archaeology "found the Tower of Babel". Say: "ang ziggurat ng Babilonya, ang Etemenanki, ang madalas iugnay ng mga iskolar sa kuwento."
  - The ziggurat we know is from the Neo-Babylonian rebuild (7th–6th century BC). It is not dated to the time of Genesis.
  - Do not add words to "ang Panginoon" beyond v6–7.
- **Verdict:** usable. Quote the text exactly and give the archaeology as what scholars link, not as proof.

## 3. The Watchers, Mount Hermon and the Nephilim
- **The text (Genesis 6:1–4, Ang Dating Biblia):**
  - v2: "Na nakita ng mga anak ng Dios, na magaganda ang mga anak na babae ng mga tao…"
  - v4: "Ang mga higante ay nasa lupa ng mga araw na yaon… ang mga ito rin ang naging makapangyarihan nang unang panahon na mga lalaking bantog."
  - Genesis does **not** use the word "Watchers", does not name Hermon, and does not say the "anak ng Dios" were angels. That reading comes from later texts.
  - Numbers 13:33 (the spies' report) mentions the Nephilim again. WP notes that report may be exaggeration from fear.
- **The Book of Enoch** (1 Enoch; WP "Book of Enoch", "Watcher (angel)", "Mount Hermon"):
  - The *Book of the Watchers* (1 Enoch 6–36) says **200** Watchers, led by **Samyaza** (Shemihaza), came down on **Mount Hermon** and swore an oath there (1 Enoch 6). They took human wives and taught forbidden knowledge; their children were the giants.
  - The oldest parts are dated **about 300–200 BC**, and the Book of Parables **about 100 BC**. It survives whole only in Ge'ez (Ethiopic); Aramaic fragments were found at Qumran.
  - The Epistle of Jude quotes it (Jude 14–15, citing "Enoch, the seventh from Adam").
- **Say it plainly:**
  - **Most churches and most of Judaism do not count 1 Enoch as scripture.**
  - The Ethiopian and Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Churches and Beta Israel do count it.
  - In the script, Enoch is "isang lumang aklat na hindi kasama sa Biblia ng karamihan ng simbahan", never "sabi ng Biblia".
- **The word:** the meaning of *nephilim* is uncertain. The Septuagint renders it *gigantes* (giants), and Ang Dating Biblia has "higante". Some scholars derive it from *n-p-l* ("to fall"). Give both if it comes up.
- **Mount Hermon:** a real mountain cluster at the south end of the Anti-Lebanon range. Its summit (2,814 m) is on the Syria–Lebanon border, and it is the highest point of Syria. The UN keeps a post on top. It is a **contested border area today**, so show it as a mountain only, with no flags, troops or present-day politics.
- **Traps:**
  - Keep three things apart: what Genesis says, what Enoch adds (not scripture for most), and what is the story's fiction.
  - Never give the Watchers' oath as biblical.
- **Verdict:** usable with the three-way label.

## 4. Atlantis (Plato, *Timaeus* and *Critias*)
- **Source:** WP "Atlantis", "Timaeus (dialogue)", "Critias (dialogue)".
- **What Plato wrote:**
  - Atlantis appears **only** in Plato's dialogues *Timaeus* and *Critias* (about **360 BC**); all later mentions come from these two.
  - In them, Critias tells a tale said to come from **Solon**, who heard it from Egyptian priests. In the tale, Atlantis was a naval power beyond the "Pillars of Heracles" that fought ancient Athens **9,000 years** before Solon, then sank in a single day and night.
  - The *Critias* is **unfinished**: it stops just as Zeus begins to speak about punishing the Atlanteans.
- **What scholars say:**
  - Present-day classicists agree the story is **fiction**, an allegory on the pride of nations.
  - They still debate what inspired it (the Thera eruption, the Sea Peoples, and others).
  - The idea of Atlantis as real history comes from 19th-century amateurs, chiefly **Ignatius Donnelly**, *Atlantis: The Antediluvian World* (1882).
- **Traps:**
  - Say "isang kuwentong isinulat ni Plato", not "isang lungsod na lumubog".
  - Don't put Atlantis on a real map as fact.
  - The "Egyptian records" are part of Plato's story, not documents anyone has found.
- **Verdict:** usable as Plato's tale, labelled.

## 5. Agartha
- **Source:** WP "Agartha" (well sourced; it relies on the occult historian J. M. Greer and others).
- **Who first wrote of it:**
  - The French writer and colonial official **Louis Jacolliot**, in an **1873** book, invented "Asgartha". He presented it as an ancient Indian capital, **not** an underground kingdom, and claimed Brahmin manuscripts as his source. WP says he "probably made the concept up himself". The name is likely from the Norse *Asgard*.
  - **Alexandre Saint-Yves d'Alveydre** (1886) made it an **underground** realm. He recalled and destroyed almost all copies of his book within two weeks; it was reissued in 1910.
  - **Ferdynand Ossendowski**, *Beasts, Men and Gods* (1922), made it widely known ("Agharti", the "King of the World"). Some scholars call his borrowing from Saint-Yves near-plagiarism.
  - **René Guénon** later wrote a book on it as the world's spiritual centre (year not given in the article read).
  - The link to the **hollow Earth** came later (Walter Siegmeister writing as "Raymond Bernard", 1960).
- **Not ancient:** no form of the name is used before the 1870s, although claims of an ancient origin are common.
- **A warning for the series:** in the 2020s Agartha became an internet meme heavily tied to neo-Nazi and "Aryan" imagery. WP also notes the 1990s Order of the Solar Temple murder-suicides drew on it.
  - The show must **avoid** Nordic "master race" imagery, black suns, "vril" or swastika-like symbols in any Agartha frame.
  - Show it as a 19th-century European invention.
- **Verdict:** usable only as "isang ideyang inimbento sa Europa noong 1870s", with the imagery warning.

## 6. The yeti and the "hairy giants" of many countries
- **Sources:** WP "Yeti", "Bigfoot", "Yeren"; the Almas article was redirected and not read, so treat it as **[not read]**.
- **Yeti (Himalaya):**
  - Folklorists trace it to Sherpa folklore plus animals mistaken for something else (bears, yaks, langurs).
  - The name is linked to Tibetan words for "rocky place" and "bear". Other names include *meh-teh*, *dzu-teh* (the Himalayan brown bear) and *bun manchi* ("jungle man", Nepali).
  - **Eric Shipton's** footprint photographs on the 1951 Everest expedition started the Western craze. The 1954 "scalp" hair was tested and was not from a yeti.
  - DNA: a sample claimed as anomalous (Sykes) was later shown, in a **2017** mtDNA study, to come from a **Himalayan brown bear**. Other "yeti" samples were from the Tibetan blue bear, the Asiatic black bear and a **dog**.
- **Bigfoot / Sasquatch (North America):**
  - Folklore and a pop-culture icon since the mid-20th century. The evidence is called "dubious".
  - In 2002 the family of Ray Wallace revealed the carved wooden feet he had used to fake the famous 1958 tracks.
- **Yeren (China):** reports of "hairy men" in Hubei (Shennongjia) are said to go back to about 340 BC; it is a cryptid with no physical proof.
- **Philippines:** the **kapre** (Ep 14) belongs in the same family of tale.
- **Trap:** say "kuwentong-bayan sa maraming bansa; walang patunay na may ganitong nilalang." Never call DNA results "inconclusive" when the 2017 study identified the animals.
- **Verdict:** usable as folklore, with the science line.

## 7. Mount Banahaw and Hermano Pule's Cofradía
- **Sources:** WP "Mount Banahaw", "Hermano Pule".
- **Mount Banahaw:**
  - An active stratovolcano complex in Quezon and Laguna, 2,170 m high. A 1730 eruption is believed to have breached the summit caldera; a lake filled the crater before 1730.
  - It is a **holy mountain** to many (*bundok dambana*), with springs whose water is treated as holy water at sites called *puestos*. Holy Week pilgrimages are large.
  - It is a protected landscape (Mounts Banahaw–San Cristobal).
- **Hermano Pule (Apolinario de la Cruz), 22 July 1815 – 4 November 1841:**
  - Born in Lucban, Tayabas (now Quezon).
  - In **December 1832**, at 18, he founded the *Cofradía de San José* with the *indio* secular priest Ciriaco de los Santos and 19 others. The brotherhood answered the racially discriminatory practices of the Church in the colony. Its rule **excluded Spaniards and mestizos**.
  - It met on the 19th of each month and had **about 4,500 to 5,000 members** by 1841.
  - Raid of **19 October 1840**: 243 members arrested. His petitions to the Archbishop and the Real Audiencia (June 1841) were refused.
  - **23 October 1841**: the cofradía, about 4,000 strong at Isabang on the slopes of Banahaw, beat back Alcalde Joaquín Ortega (Ortega was killed). Pule moved to **Alitao**, where his followers crowned him "King of the Tagalogs".
  - **1 November 1841**: Col. Joaquín Huet came with 800 to 1,000 soldiers and broke the camp; **many were killed** (WP gives no single number).
  - Pule was captured in Sariaya, given a summary trial and executed by firing squad on **4 November 1841** at age 26. His body was quartered, and his head, hands and feet were displayed. About 200 other members were executed the same day.
  - Revived in 1870–71 under Juanario Labios. The Banahaw religious groups today count him among their founders.
- **Traps:**
  - The brief's "Cofradía of 1841": the Cofradía was **founded 1832**; **1841** is the year of the rising and the massacre. Say both.
  - The story that he was refused entry to a religious order for being an *indio* is widely told but was **not found in the WP article read**. Mark it **[unverified]** until a source is opened.
  - Show the execution with restraint (no displayed body parts in frames).
- **Verdict:** keep, with the 1832/1841 fix.

## 8. Dante's *Inferno*
- **Source:** WP "Inferno (Dante)", "Malebolge".
- **Structure:**
  - Hell has **nine** concentric circles, going deeper by wickedness, down to Satan bound at the earth's centre.
  - The seventh circle has three rings. The eighth, **Malebolge** ("evil ditches"), has **ten** ditches (*bolgie*) for simple fraud. The ninth is the frozen Cocytus, for traitors.
- **Corrupt officials:** in the **eighth circle, fifth bolgia**, the **barrators** ("corrupt politicians who made money by trafficking in public offices") are sunk in a lake of **boiling pitch**. Demons called the **Malebranche** ("Evil Claws") guard them and hook anyone who surfaces. Their leader is Malacoda.
  - The **simoniacs** (those who sold church offices) are the third bolgia; WP calls the barrators their political counterpart.
- **For the series:** our story's **seven depths are not Dante's count**, and the script must say so ("hindi ito ang siyam na bilog ni Dante").
  - Dante's poem (about 1308–1320) [the date is from general knowledge, not checked in the article read; check before using] is a **poem**, not scripture and not doctrine. Label it so.
- **Trap:** don't say "the Bible says there are nine circles". The circles are Dante's.
- **Verdict:** usable as literature, labelled.

## 9. The flaming sword (Genesis 3:24)
- **The text (Ang Dating Biblia):** "Ano pa't itinaboy ang lalake; at inilagay sa silanganan ng halamanan ng Eden ang mga Querubin at ang isang nagniningas na tabak na umiikot, upang ingatan ang daang patungo sa kahoy ng buhay."
- **What it says and does not say:**
  - The sword is placed **with** the cherubim, at the **east** of the garden. It **turns every way** ("umiikot"). Its purpose is to guard the way to the tree of life.
  - The Hebrew is *lahat hachereb hammithappeket*, "the flame of the whirling sword".
  - Genesis does **not** say a cherub holds it in a hand. WP says scholars read it variously: a weapon of the cherubim, lightning, a metaphor, an independent object.
  - Eastern Orthodox tradition (the Lenten Triodion) says it was removed after the Resurrection. That is a tradition, not the Genesis text.
- **Trap:** quote the verse exactly; anything more is "sa tradisyon" or "sa ating kuwento".
- **Verdict:** usable.

## 10. The war in heaven (Revelation 12)
- **The text (Pahayag 12:7–9, Ang Dating Biblia):**
  - v7: "At nagkaroon ng pagbabaka sa langit: si Miguel at ang kaniyang mga anghel ay nakipagbaka sa dragon; at ang dragon at ang kaniyang mga anghel ay nakipagbaka;"
  - v8: "At hindi sila nanganalo, ni nasumpungan pa man ang kanilang dako sa langit."
  - v9: "At inihagis ang malaking dragon, ang matandang ahas, ang tinatawag na Diablo at Satanas, ang dumadaya sa buong sanglibutan; siya'y inihagis sa lupa, at ang kaniyang mga anghel ay inihagis na kasama niya."
- **What comes from later tradition** (WP "War in Heaven"):
  - The name "Lucifer" for Satan comes from linking Isaiah 14 with this passage.
  - The detailed story of a rebellion before creation, with angels choosing sides, is largely from later tradition and from **Milton's *Paradise Lost*** (1667), which Doré illustrated in 1866.
  - Revelation itself is apocalyptic writing, and its **timing** (past, present or future) is read differently by different churches.
- **Traps:**
  - Quote only what the verses say: Miguel, his angels, the dragon cast down to earth.
  - Don't put "Lucifer" or Milton's scenes in the Bible's mouth.
  - God speaks no invented line.
- **Verdict:** usable.

## Summary of traps for the Season 3 scripts
1. Keep these apart, in this order of authority: **Scripture (quote exactly)**, then **later religious texts not counted as scripture by most churches** (1 Enoch), then **literature** (Plato, Dante, Milton), then **folklore** (Biringan, the kapre, the yeti), then **modern invention** (Agartha, 1873/1886), then **our fiction**.
2. Dates to use: Cofradía **1832**, rising and massacre **1841**, Pule executed **4 November 1841**; Plato **about 360 BC**; 1 Enoch's oldest parts **about 300–200 BC**; Jacolliot **1873**; Saint-Yves **1886**; Shipton **1951**.
3. No present-day flags or politics on Mount Hermon. No Nazi-adjacent imagery for Agartha. Restraint on the 1841 executions.
4. Still to open before a `facts.md` uses them: the Almas article; a source for Pule being refused by a religious order; the date of Dante's *Inferno*.
