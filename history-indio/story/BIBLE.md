# INDIO: ANG SAKSI — Series Bible v2

14 November 2026. This is the story law for all 50 episodes from now on. It sits on top of `SERIES.md` (canon and
guard rails stay) and answers `story/CONSISTENCY.md` (what was broken). Where this file and an old script
disagree, this file wins and `REWRITE_PLAN.md` says what changes in that script.

How it was made: three writers proposed alone, each through one lens (the short-drama engine; the mystery web;
the role-playing game): `story/_parts/proposal_A.md`, `proposal_B.md`, `proposal_C.md`. The curator took the
strongest part of each and judged the result three ways (the owner's brief; a first-time viewer of any age;
truth and the guard rails): section 13.

## 0. The form of every episode (the owner's ruling)

1. **Scenes, not narration.** We watch in the third person. Each scene is written like this:

   ```
   TAGAPAGSALAYSAY: Agosto 1896. Isang kamalig sa labas ng Maynila.
   (Hinila si Indio ng dalawang lalaki. Itinutok sa kanya ang itak.)
   ISLAW: Nasaan ang cedula mo?
   INDIO: (bulong, sa atin) Sabi sa libro, pinunit nila. Walang nagsabing hahanapan ako.
   ```

   One short storyteller line sets place and time. What people do is in (parentheses) and is what the picture
   shows. Then they talk. No stretch of more than two storyteller lines in a row; no talking heads.
2. **Indio does not know the history.** He finds it out there, from a **friend he makes in that time or place**
   (always an invented, ordinary person: a child, a porter, a cook). What he brings from today is only the
   **sabi-sabi**: what people say now ("Sabi sa TikTok...", "Sabi ng titser ko..."). He whispers it to us, then
   sees what is in front of him. That gap is where the truth of the episode is told, by sight, with a joke.
3. **A reason drives every scene** (he wants one thing) and the scene ends when he gets it, loses it, or it
   turns into something else. A scene with no want is cut.
4. **One plot, one twist, and the twist runs through a fall**: he seems to LOSE; he comes back by one of his own
   gifts; then he may show off big. No kind of twist more than four times in the 50, never twice in a row.
5. **The real outcome of history stands** at the end of the scene in the past. He never changes what happened;
   what he wins is small, human and invented (a child reaches a boat; a lie is not believed by one person).
6. **Nothing in the story says a part is invented.** No "kathang-isip" block, no "tiyak / pinagtatalunan"
   lecture at the end. (The three words survive only as a move inside the plot: the hearing of Episode 37, the
   battles of 44 and 48.) Uncertainty is shown as Usman's growl inside the scene: **u-MU-ngol**.
7. **The trivia goes at the END**, after the last line of the story, as "Patalastas ni Usman": the true,
   sourced facts of the episode's real moment, with what is disputed. The fiction label lives in the caption
   and the pinned comment only.
8. **No generic follow card.** The last line of the story is the cliffhanger. The only ask is the question of
   the episode, which is always a choice the viewer would have to make in Indio's place.
9. **Nothing is seen twice.** Each episode has its own way in (not always a marker), its own first line, its
   own job for Usman, its own ending shape. "Ako 'to si Indio." stays as the signature, said once, wherever it
   lands hardest (not always second). "Samahan mo akong mag-time travel" is dropped.
10. Tone for all ages: a hero who is laughed at, a talking dog, monsters that turn out to be guards, a family
    to find. Vice, romance and death by suggestion only, as `SERIES.md` already rules.

## 1. The one question

**"Ano ang nakita ng aso, at bakit si Indio lang ang nakakarinig sa kanya?"**

Asked in Episode 1 (a dog out of the light knows the name of a man nobody will hire). Sharpened in 4 (he was
Rizal's dog), in 6 (the medallion is gone and Indio still hears him), in 22 (every tongue breaks and the dog is
still clear), in 30 (he was in the depths before they had names). Answered in 48: Usman was in the field with
Abel. He is the one witness of the first lie; he has walked every age looking for someone who can hear an
animal; every u-MU-ngol in fifty episodes was that witness knowing a lie. "Sila" exist to keep the first lie
standing. A witness who can hear the dog can end it.

What the viewer feels episode by episode is the short-drama version of the same thing: **when will everybody
see that the man they called "tanso lang" is the one true thing, and what will it cost him?**

| Season | Its question |
|---|---|
| 1, Pilipinas (Ep 1-8) | Bakit ako? At sino ang babaeng may pulang payong na nagliligaw sa akin? |
| 2, Asya at ang mga dagat (9-20) | Kapag nasa akin na ang lahat, may matitira pa bang Indio? At sino ang laging nauuna? |
| 3, ang mga tagong lugar (21-31) | Kung patay ang lahat ng gamit, ano ang akin talaga? |
| 4, ang puwesto at ang baha (32-41) | Kaya bang manalo ng saksi sa lugar na kuwento ang sandata? |
| 5, Digmaan sa Langit (42-50) | Ano ang gagawin ng saksi sa kaaway na nakaluhod na? |

## 2. Indio's own gifts: "Ang Tatlong Likas"

The medallions are **equipment**: they can be taken, forged, bought, or go cold. The gifts are **Indio**. For
twenty-nine episodes he and the viewer believe the power is in the bronze, the lens, the lamp. The villains
say "ilusyon lang". The series is the slow proof, to a man who believes he is nothing, that the bronze only
ever warmed because of him. He never names a gift himself: Usman names them, to tease him.

| Gift | What it does | Rule | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ang Dinig** | He understands animals: Usman first, then any animal, then the beings. | *"Tumahimik ka muna."* Only while he himself is quiet inside: not drunk, not boasting, not lying. Animals never lie, but they know only what they smelled, heard and feared: no dates, no names. | *"Walang libre sa hayop."* The animal is paid first, from his own share (his food, his water, his jacket). He goes hungry a lot: the running joke. And for a few breaths after, human speech is noise to him. |
| **Ang Malas** (ang pagmamalas) | One true detail stands out for him: the hand that shakes, the brick that is new, the person missing from the table. | *"Huwag kang pipikit."* (M's line at Bagumbayan becomes the rule.) It shows only what is in front of him now and never what it means; the meaning he must get from people. It goes blind when he looks for himself (greed, drink, pride). | *"Tatlong hingang dilim."* At full strength the world holds still for three breaths while he looks, and he may touch nothing; then he is blind for three breaths. The fall of an episode often happens in that dark. (This is the "time stop" of Episodes 25, 38 and 44, now with a rule.) |
| **Ang Udyok** | A person in front of him loses his excuse: he confesses, or at last does the thing he was afraid to do. Its spoken key is the series' line: **"Nakita na kita."** | *"Ako muna."* He must first say aloud one true thing against himself that costs him. It never forces: the person still chooses. It is dead while a lie is in his mouth, and on a promise he cannot keep. | Everyone present hears his confession, and a truth once spent never works again: every use needs a deeper one. He runs out of cheap truths in Season 2. |

**The pun that carries the underdog.** From Episode 1 everyone says *"Malas ka talaga, Indio"* (unlucky). In
Episode 29 his father gives the old meaning: *"Hindi ka malas, anak. Nagmamalas ka."* The insult becomes the
title.

**The joined gift, Ang Saksi:** hear, see, say, in one breath. Only twice: Episode 37 ("Saksi ako.") and 48.

**The marks are levels, and a level comes only from a loss.** A marka is not a count of jumps. It burns in when
he keeps his eyes open on a truth that costs him. Episodes 1 to 5: one each (the job, the pay, his pride, his
only friend, his last ₱20). Episode 6: none, because at the volley he shuts his eyes; that is why he is
stranded. Episode 8: the sixth (he sees himself, and stays). Episodes 9 to 19: none; he was rich and nothing
cost him. Episode 20: the seventh, when he gives up the prize to pull his rival out of the water. Seven marks,
seven depths, seven heads of the dragon. They vanish in Episode 49. (Every count already in the videos is
kept; only the reason is new.)

## 3. The rival: Rico Bulan, "ang tunay na saksi"

Everybody's idea of what a witness should look like. Federico "Rico" Bulan: first honor, a degree on the wall,
a ring light, millions of followers on his history channel, perfect teeth, perfect papers. "Si Rico nga may
diploma. Ikaw, ano'ng natapos mo?" He is not a monster. He believes his own press. That is why he is a rival
and not a villain, and why he can come back.

- **Not near him.** Until Episode 44 they are almost never in the same room: Rico is a face on a screen, a
  convoy that left an hour ago, a name on a tarpaulin.
- **First seen, unnamed.** Ep 1: a smiling face on the tarpaulin at the shrine, "Heritage Walk kasama si ...",
  the name under a stapled flyer; the tour job Indio was refused went to him. Ep 2: the app says "Top Rider of
  History Tours: R.B., five stars." Ep 10: the Grade 5 boy who recites the false Code of Kalantiaw perfectly and
  takes the medal while the boy Indio stands in the corridor for saying it is false (we do not join the boy and
  the tarpaulin yet). Ep 12: a hand with a ring light in a pickup window; tyre tracks in the year 900. Ep 14: a
  vlog with no face. **Named in Ep 17**, live in London: "Rico Bulan po, ang inyong tunay na saksi." Ep 19, Ma'am
  Celia on the phone: "Si Federico? Kaklase mo 'yon. Siya ang may medalya." The scene of Episode 10 changes its
  meaning.
- **His "power" is the real illusion.** In Ep 1 the pawnbroker's helper presses the Bracken medallion into wax
  before handing it back ("tanso lang 'yan"). From that print Tubal, a smith, casts **Ang Huwad**, a forged
  door. It carries its holder into the past badly: he can stay only minutes, it leaves rust on what he
  touches, and it shows him only what he already wants to see. Rico has never once truly seen the past. His
  "party" is caught, not won: beings in steel nets and collars, shown to cheering crowds. Under every video of
  Indio he types: **"Ilusyon lang."** (This also answers who of "sila" can travel: Ep 12, Ep 17.)
- **How he "beats" Indio, never the same way twice.** Ep 1 the job. Ep 10 the medal. Ep 12 he is there first
  and drives off with the chest. Ep 17 he "finds" the 1762 crate on camera. Ep 19 three million views:
  "magnanakaw ng relikya". Ep 20 he lifts the twelfth medallion first. Ep 21 Samar welcomes his convoy. Ep 31 by
  evening he is the hero of a rescue Indio made. Ep 33 he is the paid "heritage consultant" of Tubal's wall.
  Ep 35 the forged deed. Ep 39 he hosts the debate. Ep 41 "siya ang gumawa ng baha".
- **The reversal, in five beats.** (1) Ep 20: in Rico's hand the Kalasag stays cold, "tanso lang"; the siyokoy
  take him; Indio gives up the prize to pull him out, and the shield wakes on Indio's arm. Rico thanks nobody.
  (2) Ep 37: under oath Rico is asked one plain question and cannot name one thing he saw himself. (3) Ep 40:
  Indio leaves Rico's name on the ark list. (4) Ep 44: Ang Huwad cracks on a live video; his collared beasts
  turn on him; Indio shields him; through Sulayman's window Rico sees the true 1570, the first real sight of
  his life, and turns the camera on himself and says nothing. (5) Ep 48: when the man in white says nobody
  stands with a witness, the first voice is Rico's: "Saksi ako. Sa kanya." He is the one who holds the Tabò for
  the wounded Indio. Ep 49: he tells a child the truth, unasked. The false witness is the one thing Indio did
  change, and it is the answer to "wala kang binago".

## 5. Allies won: "Mga Kakampi"

*Buno*: it tests him, and he may not wound it. Here he seems to lose.
*Buhol*: he hears it (Dinig), sees what it truly is (Malas), says "Nakita na kita" (Udyok); M's medallion, Ang
Sandugo, warms; and the being ties itself as **one knot on the red cord** M cut from her umbrella for his wrist.
Limits: only **three knots** can be lit in one fight, so every call is a choice; a called ally stays for one
task; each asks its **upa** (wage) every time. When M is away (Ep 14-15) a creature can only give its word.

## 6. The healing item: **Ang Tabò ng Bukal**

A coconut-shell dipper, cracked and tied with rattan. In Episode 24 the queen of the sunken city offers a
golden cup; the cup is the trap. Behind the throne the Pugita is chained over the city's first spring, and
floating there is the dipper the first people drank from before there was gold. Indio: "Tabò? 'Yung
pampaligo?" The humblest thing in the series heals everything.

- **Won from:** the boss of Episode 24 (Sinag's city, the Pugita).
- **Rule:** one *salok* heals anything: a wound, a fever, poison, a cold medallion, a memory.
- **Limits:** (1) one salok, then it is dry until the next new moon; (2) **it never heals the one who holds
  it**: somebody else must hold it for you; (3) it does not raise the dead; (4) it cannot heal what the sick one
  will not admit; (5) it cannot break a law or a bargain (so it cannot empty Biringan).
- **The times it matters:**
  1. Ep 25: Usman is fading in the snow, or the yeti's trapped foot. He gives it to the yeti. An enemy healed
     becomes an ally; Usman holds on by himself.

## 8. The role-playing frame, on screen, without a menu

- **Party.** Three fixed seats and one guest seat. **Indio**, ang Saksi. **Usman**, ang Ilong: he growls at a
  lie and names Indio's gifts to mock them. **M**, ang Bantay: she seals and keeps the rule; she never fights
  for him. The fourth seat is **the friend of that time**, who teaches one small thing (a knot, a whistle, a
  way to carry water, a shirt worn inside out) that pays off later. The barkada of today have jobs: Bea
  (Tagasulat), JM (Mekaniko), Ma'am Celia (Guro), Totoy (Kapitan, from Ep 40).
- **Quest.** One line near the top, never a narrator's: an order slip, Lola's text, a line the notebook
  writes, a dare from Rico's video. Usman may turn it into a joke: "Misyon, par: huwag kang malunod."

  handed over, the three dark breaths, the confession said first.
- **Levels.** The marks (section 2). How a level is *felt*: (a) the thing that beat him last time is done in
  one beat this time, and nobody remarks on it; (b) what strangers call him changes: tambay, indio, magnanakaw,
  kap, mayor, sugo, pastol; (c) each season's boss asks the same question, **"Sino ka ba?"**, and the answer is
  the level: Ep 8 "Wala. Tambay lang." Ep 20 "Indio." Ep 30 "Saksi lang ako." Ep 37 "Saksi ako." Ep 50 "Ako 'to
  si Indio."
- **Gear** is lit or cold. "Cold" is the fall made visible. The **hologram** appears at most once an episode,

  slots, the knots, **KAALAMAN %** ending in "?". The percentage rises only when he finds something out there,

## 9. Hard rules that end the contradictions

1. **Marks:** section 2. Lola's line in Ep 21 drops the number.
2. **The family medallion has one origin: the loop.** Hung on the child in 1898, it comes down to Lolo Isidro,
   who leaves it for Indio. In the family's story Lolo Pepe traced **the other eleven**, on paper.
3. **The star never leaves the handle of M's umbrella** until Episode 46. He makes the first wish with his
   hand on the handle, by accident (Ep 11). Ep 41 does not say where the hilt is; Ep 46 reveals that the handle
   is the hilt.
4. **Three words:** *ang mapa* (later *ang susi*); *ang tansong kasulatan* (the copperplate); *Ang Kasulatan*
   (the medallion only). Twelve dots: ten medallions to find and two lessons (Galilee, the school), said once
   in Ep 9.
5. **Cold and warm:** all medallions go cold in Ep 14 and re-warm one by one, on screen, from the pandesal of
   Ep 15. He reads Kawi only after Ang Kasulatan. Lihim and Tinig are never used in one breath.
6. **The money:** one number, YAMAN, on the hologram from Ep 11, falling. The fifty are bought in Ep 11-14,
   locked in the garage in Ep 15 (they are in JM's name: he cannot give them away), impounded in 35, returned
   in 37, given away in 38 with the last of the wish. From Ep 15 he pays his way by work.
7. **Usman** has a body only where time is open: a jump, a lagusan, Biringan, Babel, the depths. He is never
   in a present-day scene before the blade of Episode 30; from then he stays. (Ep 13 s57 and Ep 20 s51 go.)
8. **The door.** The door was always the reading aloud; the bronze was the handle. After Ep 30 he needs no
   medallion and still needs a marker; he goes forward only by the Orasan. He can never alter what he
   witnessed. He acts in the present with what he saw (Ep 38: the sabotage becomes evidence). The third wish
   does not rewrite our past: it opens the other garden.
9. **Names.** Lola Ising, Lolo Isidro and Ma'am Celia are named at first sight. Indio says **"Papa"**; adults
   say "Teddy"; "Teodoro" is only on paper. The flood wall "on paper" is in the invented town of **San
   Lorenzo** in every episode; Kawit has only the flood. Episode 21 is retitled **"Ang Ate sa Hamog"**.
10. **Biringan's three conditions** are each met on screen in Ep 30: the blade cuts the lie "kusa silang
    kumain"; Albo comes to the feather and gives his consent; the Daan Pauwi carries only one who has a table
    waiting. The mother's line becomes: "Sabi ko sa'yo, uuwi tayo."
11. **The flood** comes at 100% because the enemy moves when the witness is complete. Indio did not make it.
    It is the dragon's water (Revelation 12:15), never God's (Genesis 9:11, always read as "Nakasulat...").
12. **The last enemy** is seen from Ep 11 and named by M inside Ep 48. He is not Sinag. The dragon is driven
    off by M's host, off Indio's screen; the princes fall silent one by one; Tubal drops the ring.
13. **M's choice.** In Ep 47 she lays down her place in the host to stay; her light leaves the hologram; from
    then she can be wounded. Nobody above says it is right, and nobody above speaks. The summons of Ep 45 is
    her own request: "Kailangan kita roon. Hindi sa laban namin. Sa laban mo."
14. **Ages** are shown, not announced: Totoy's height, a few grey hairs. Said aloud at most at 25, 35 and 40.
