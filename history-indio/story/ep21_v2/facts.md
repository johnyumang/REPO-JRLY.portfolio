There is no dated real moment in this episode. The "real part" is (a) that Biringan is a folk tale of Samar,
told as a tale, and (b) the origin of the word "adobo".

## 1. What is real in this script, and where it is said

| Line (sentences.json) | Claim | Status and sources |
|---|---|---|
| s13 to s14 (INDIO) | "Adobo: Kastila ang pangalan, galing sa 'adobar', ibabad. Pero ang pagluluto sa suka, atin na bago pa sila dumating." | **Supported, two sources.** (1) Wikipedia "Philippine adobo": the name is "from Spanish adobar: 'marinade', 'sauce' or 'seasoning'"; "The cooking method for the Philippine adobo is indigenous to the Philippines"; the food historian Raymond Sokolov held that the name "was applied to a native dish that already existed before the arrival of the Spanish" (cited there to Pia Lim-Castillo, 2006). (2) Wiktionary, Tagalog "adobo": from Spanish "adobo", from "adobar", to marinate [summary only]. "Ibabad" is our gloss of "to marinate". **One dissent, kept out of the story and noted here:** Doreen Fernandez argued that adobo may have been indigenized from the Mexican adobo (same article). The trivia therefore says "ayon sa mga historyador ng pagkain" |
| s15 to s16 (INDIO) | "Pinangalanan lang nila. Parang ako: Indio." | The joke. "Indio" was the Spanish colonial word for the native people of these islands (the series' own premise since Episode 1). No new claim |
| s27 (PACING) | "Ayon sa kuwento naming mga taga-Samar, diyan ang Biringan: lungsod ng mga engkanto, hindi nakikita." | **Folklore, certain that it is told.** Wikipedia "Biringan": a "mythical city said to lie invisibly" in Samar, peopled by engkanto; the name is glossed there from Waray as "the place where the lost are found" (one source, not spoken). The article puts the tale between Gandara and Pagsanghan; **no real town is named in the script**, and Nanay Pacing's "diyan" points at a fog bank off an invented shore |
| s28 (PACING) | "At sabi ng matatanda: ang kumain sa hapag nila, hindi na nakakauwi." | **A common motif of engkanto tales across the Philippines, not specific to Biringan**: said with "sabi ng matatanda", and the trivia says "hindi lang sa Samar". Vogue Philippines, quoting Karl Gaverza: "If a human eats black rice in the engkanto or engkanta's domain they can never return home" [summary only]; Demetrio 1968 describes the belief as found in Luzon, the Visayas and Mindanao [summary only]. Wikipedia "Biringan" gives NO eating rule for Biringan itself; the story's "law of the city" is invented on top of the general motif (section 2) |
| s29 (INDIO) | "Sabi sa internet, nangunguha raw sila ng tao." | The episode's sabi-sabi, said as hearsay. Wikipedia "Biringan" reports stories of people "taken" or possessed; nothing in the script confirms it, and what he sees is the opposite (a guard) |
| Trivia | Biringan is a folk tale of Samar; the stories spread from the 1960s; no map proves it | Wikipedia "Biringan": "Folklore about the city began circulating in the 1960s." One source: the trivia says "ayon sa mga tala" |
| Trivia | Pedro de San Buenaventura's dictionary of 1613 calls it "adobo de los naturales" | Wikipedia "Philippine adobo": the term "was first recorded in the 1613 dictionary Vocabulario de la lengua tagala, compiled by the Spanish Franciscan missionary Pedro de San Buenaventura", as "adobo de los naturales" (cited there to Serna Estrella 2013 and Fernandez, Tikim). **One source read; the 1613 page itself was not seen**: check before the build, or drop the year from the trivia |

**Hero line: none.** No real figure of history appears or is named in the story. No hero's partner, so no
tender beat. No scripture. No diwa speaks.

## 3. Guard rails checked for this script

- Biringan is carried by the friend as a tale ("ayon sa kuwento naming mga taga-Samar"; "sabi ng matatanda"),
  never by the storyteller as a fact. No real town or living person of Samar is named, shown or blamed. The
  people of the baryo are hosts; the one who warns and feeds him is one of them.
- The engkanto are given no faces, no words and no malice on screen: the only one who comes out of the fog is
  his sister. "Nangunguha ng tao" is Indio's hearsay and is not confirmed.
- No living person, firm, app or brand ("sabi sa internet"). Rico Bulan is invented and is not in the room.
- No sacred rite, no scripture, no word from God. No sentence says anything is invented. Trivia at the end.
  No follow card.
- Usman is only at the open lagusan and the fog (time open); the kitchen, the plaza and the Lola's kitchen
  have no dog.
- "He confessed, so it worked" is not used. The comeback is his own eye, by the friend's rule (the hand, not
  the face), and the note he has carried since Acapulco.
- No mark, medallion or knot is gained or lost. The hologram is seen once, three seconds, unread (KAALAMAN a
  little higher). One knot is used (the second), within the three-knot limit; its wage is said.
- The mother has no voice in this episode (her call is reported in s11); the father is only in the Lola's
  last line.
