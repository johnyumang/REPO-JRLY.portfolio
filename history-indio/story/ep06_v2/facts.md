# Facts — Episode 6 (version 2): Ang Huling Umaga (Bagumbayan, 30 Disyembre 1896)

Built on `videos/ep06-ang-huling-umaga/facts.md` (checked 2026-11-12; search summaries, cross-checked). New in
version 2 (web search, 15 Nov 2026, summaries only): the origin of taho; the reported last words; Josephine
Bracken's farewell and the book.

## 1. What is real in this script, and where it is said

| Line (sentences.json) | Claim | Status and sources |

| s12 to s13 (INDIO) | "Taho: galing sa salitang Tsino para sa tokwa. Dinala rito ng mga mangangalakal, tapos nilagyan natin ng arnibal at sago." | Well supported, not from a dictionary: taho is the Philippine form of Chinese douhua (soft tofu); the word is traced to Philippine Hokkien 豆腐 "tāu-hū" (tofu). Wikipedia, "Douhua" and "Dubu/Tofu" (Philippine section); Wiktionary, 豆腐 (descendants). Romanisations differ. "Mga mangangalakal" is the common account of how Chinese foods arrived; arnibal and sago are how it is sold today |

## 3. Guard rails checked for this script

- One real person speaks two Latin words: Rizal, his reported last words. He is a far figure in black, no
  likeness, never in close-up at the moment of death. Josephine Bracken is not shown as a person: only two
  hands and a book.
- The death is not shown: black screen, the sound, birds, a hat on the grass. No body, no blood, no soldier's
  face in close-up. The trivia after this episode is the quiet version.
- Indio speaks to no real person and changes nothing. The story does not say he is Rizal's kin.
- No living person; the "named once, kindly" rule is not used. No vendor, brand or stall is shown for the taho.
- No sentence says anything is invented. Trivia after the last line. No follow card.
- Usman is only inside the jump; from the end of this episode he stays with Indio in the past (BIBLE 9.7).
