## 1. What is real in this script, and where it is said

| Line (sentences.json) | Claim | Status and sources |
|---|---|---|
| s11 to s12 (INDIO) | "Tiangge: sabi nila, galing sa 'tianguis' ng Mexico, at 'yon, sa 'tianquiztli' ng mga Aztec: palengke. Sumakay ng galeon ang salita." | **Well supported, two sources**, and still said with "sabi nila". (1) Wiktionary, Tagalog "tiyangge" (alternative form "tiangge"): borrowed from Mexican Spanish "tianguis" / "tiangue", from Classical Nahuatl "tiyānquiztli" (an open-air market held on a market day). (2) León-Portilla's article on Nahuatlisms in Philippine Spanish, after Retana's dictionary of 1921, lists the word (he notes older local senses: a game in Manila, a small shop in Iloilo) [summary only]. "Ng mga Aztec" stands for "of Nahuatl, the language of the Aztecs". "Sumakay ng galeon" is Indio's picture for the route (Mexico to Manila by the galleon trade); no source gives a date for the borrowing, which is why "sabi nila" stays |
| s15 to s16 (INDIO) | Mexico has its own mango, called "mango Manila" | Confirmed as a present-day name: Wikipedia "Carabao (mango)" (the Mexican cultivars Ataulfo and Manilita are called "Manila mangoes" in trade; it cites L. J. Adams, "Mexico tries to claim 'Manila mango' name as its own", San Diego Union-Tribune, 19 June 2005). The story says only that the name exists; the claim about how it got there is in the trivia (below) |
| s21 to s22 | Acapulco, 8 October 1565 | Certain. Wikipedia "Andrés de Urdaneta" ("reached Acapulco on 8 October 1565"); Wikipedia "Manila galleon" ("arriving on October 8, 1565"); a Spanish naval history paper (armada.defensa.gob.es, Cuadernos del IHCN no. 84) gives the same day [summary only]. The hour is not recorded: the afternoon light is staging |
| s24 | A ship with torn sails comes into the bay | The ship is real; "punit ang layag" is staging for a ship four months at sea |
| s25 against s30 to s34 | "Sabi ng titser ko, barko ng yaman ang galeon: seda, pilak." | The episode's sabi-sabi, true of the later trade (Chinese silk east, American silver west: Wikipedia "Manila galleon"). The first return carried only "a small cargo of spices" (Wikipedia "Andrés de Urdaneta") |
| s27 to s28 (LUPITA) | The ship is the San Pedro; it comes from the islands across the sea | Certain: the San Pedro left Cebu (Wikipedia "Andrés de Urdaneta": 1 June 1565; another account says 9 June, so no date of leaving is spoken) |
| s29 (LUPITA) | "Wala pa raw barkong nakabalik mula roon." | A child's hearsay ("raw"), true of Acapulco and of every attempt before 1565. NOT true to the week: Alonso de Arellano's San Lucas had reached Barra de Navidad in August 1565 (Wikipedia "Andrés de Urdaneta"). The trivia says so |
| s30 | Most of the men on deck are sick | Wikipedia "Andrés de Urdaneta": "Most of the crew was suffering from scurvy and only 18 remained strong enough to sail the ship" (one secondary source, Mitchell); Wikipedia "Manila galleon" says most of the crew died on the voyage; a document found in the search gives sixteen dead before anchoring. The numbers disagree, so none is spoken |
| s31 | The friar Andrés de Urdaneta, "na nagturo ng daang pabalik" | Certain that he was aboard, an Augustinian friar (from 1552), and is credited with the return route (tornaviaje). | s34 | "ang unang galeon" | The title's claim: the San Pedro's arrival opened the Manila to Acapulco trade of 1565 to 1815 (Wikipedia "Manila galleon"; Britannica "Manila galleon"). "First" is qualified in the trivia (Arellano) |
| s48 to s49 (M) | "Nahual. Taong nagiging hayop." | Folk belief of Mesoamerica, from Nahuatl "nāhualli": a person who can change into an animal; the jaguar is a known form; in parts of Oaxaca naguals are held to be protectors of the community (Wikipedia "Nagual"). Trivia: "paniniwala ng mga katutubo ng Mexico" |
| s64 (M) | Rajah Tupas of Sugbu, "kung saan galing ang barko" | Certain: Tupas was the last rajah of Sugbu and made the treaty with Legazpi in June 1565 (Wikipedia "Rajah Tupas"; "Treaty of Cebu (1565)"); the San Pedro sailed from Cebu. Tupas never went to Acapulco and the story does not say he did |
| s68 to s69 (LUPITA) | She has never seen a mango | **Consistent with the record.** The mango is Asian and reached Mexico only through the galleon trade: Wikipedia "Carabao (mango)" (the Mexican cultivars "descended from the Philippine mango through the Manila galleon trade between 1600 and 1800", citing Rocha et al., Florida Entomologist 95(1), 2012); Inquirer Opinion, "Our Mexican connection" ("Mangoes from the Philippines were introduced to Mexico where they were known as 'mangas de Manila'"). So in October 1565 there is no mango in Acapulco: the one mango comes from Indio's bag, from today |
| Trivia | 8 October 1565, from Sugbu, with Urdaneta; the trade lasted until 1815; Arellano was back in August; the mango went to Mexico by the galleon trade and is called "mango Manila" there | As above. **The mango: the two sources that REWRITE_PLAN asks for are found** (Wikipedia "Carabao (mango)" with its citations; Inquirer Opinion). That the Mexican "Manila" variety descends from galleon seedlings is commonly told and less well documented, so the trivia says "nakarating ..., ayon sa mga tala" and does not name a year |

**Hero line: none.** Andrés de Urdaneta is the significant real figure in the scene. No iconic line of his is
attested or firmly traditional, so he says nothing and is seen from far (rule 3; BIBLE section 12). No tender
beat: he was a friar. Rajah Tupas does not appear: the diwa in the medallion has one invented line under the
owner's exception, "Ang ibinahagi, dumarami." (s63). It says nothing about his own acts, puts no guilt or
belief on him, and the trivia says "ang sinabi ng diwa, hindi tala".

## 3. Guard rails checked for this script

- One real person in view, far and silent (Urdaneta); no likeness; no invented act beyond standing at the bow.
  The sick crew are shown with dignity, not as a joke: Indio's lines about them are plain.
- No sacred rite is shown. The multiplied thing is fruit, not bread (BIBLE section 12), under a bridge at
  night, with no seated crowd and no verse; it must not be staged like Episode 9.
- The children's hunger is given no cause and blamed on no real person or group. The nahual is a guard, not a
  monster; the people of Acapulco are people.
- No living person, firm or brand. "Sabi nila" and "sabi ng titser ko" name nobody. No sentence says anything
  is invented. Trivia at the end. No follow card. Usman is only inside the jump.
- "He confessed, so it worked" is not used. The comeback is the friend's rule (do not run, do not strike),
  what he sees (the ribs; where the animal stands), and his own supper given first.
- History stands: the ship arrives, the trade begins; no mango is left to grow in 1565.
