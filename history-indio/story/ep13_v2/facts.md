## 1. What is real in this script, and where it is said

| Line (sentences.json) | Claim | Status and sources |
|---|---|---|
| s10 to s12 (INDIO) | "Sabi nila, Pinoy ang nag-imbento: si Roberto del Rosario, may patent ng Sing-Along System. May mga Hapon ding nauna." | TWO SOURCES. (1) Wikipedia, Roberto del Rosario: Roberto Legaspi del Rosario (born 1919 or 1920, died 2003) developed the Sing-Along System in 1975; widely credited in the Philippines as "inventor of the karaoke"; elsewhere the credit goes to Daisuke Inoue (Juke-8, 1971) and Shigeichi Negishi (Sparko Box, 1967). (2) Supreme Court, Del Rosario v. Court of Appeals and Janito Corporation, G.R. No. 115106, 15 March 1996 (digests at jur.ph, batas.org; text at WIPO Lex): he held utility model patents UM-5269 (2 June 1983) and UM-6237 (14 November 1986) for a sing-along system. So "may patent" is certain; "Pinoy ang nag-imbento" is what is commonly said and is disputed, hence "Sabi nila" and "May mga Hapon ding nauna". He is dead, so he is named, once, kindly. No living person is named (Inoue is not). The score of 98 and the yacht are invented |
| s8, s19 | Limasawa today; a marker of the first Mass | The NHCP reaffirmed Limasawa as the site on 19 August 2020; a marker (replica unveiled 31 March 1951) and a national shrine (R.A. 2733, 1960) stand there: Wikipedia, First Mass in the Philippines. The marker's text is NOT quoted: he "reads it aloud, shouting" |
| s20 to s21 | Mazaua; Easter Sunday, 31 March 1521 | Certain: Pigafetta ("Sunday, the last of March, and Easter-day"); Wikipedia, First Mass in the Philippines; Wikipedia, Mazaua |
| s25 | The foreigners are guests of the rajahs | Pigafetta: the two rulers received and fed the fleet over several days (Wikipedia, Mazaua: "hosted Magellan's crew"). Mang Laon and his wish are invented |
| s28 | A Mass on the shore | Certain: said by the fleet's chaplain, Fr Pedro de Valderrama, near the shore. The priest is not named in the story and has no words; the trivia does not name him either |
| s29 | About fifty foreigners from the ships | Pigafetta: "we landed with about fifty men, without our body armour, but carrying our other arms, and dressed in our best clothes" (Robertson; confirmed by search summary, 2026-11-16) |
| s29 | The two rajahs, Kolambu and his brother Siagu | Pigafetta names "Raia Colambu" and "Raia Siaui" and calls them brothers. Spellings follow Wikipedia (Kolambu; Siagu). Which brother ruled where is read differently in the Italian (Robertson) and French (Skelton) texts, so the story gives no realm to either |
| s30 | Both kissed the cross and knelt | Pigafetta (Robertson): the kings "went forward to kiss the cross as we did"; at the elevation "they remained on their knees and worshiped Him with clasped hands" (both confirmed by search summary, 2026-11-16). Pigafetta adds that they did not make the offering: not said in the story; from memory of the Robertson text, to be re-read before the build |
| s31 | He hears only the waves | Staging: the rite is far, and silent for us. (Pigafetta records the ships' guns at the elevation and musk water sprinkled on the kings before the Mass; neither is shown or said) |
| s33 to s34 against s28 to s30 | "Sa araw na 'to, naging Kristiyano na tayong lahat." / "Wala akong nakitang bininyagan." | The episode's sabi-sabi. No baptism is recorded at Mazaua; the baptisms Pigafetta records came at Cebu on 14 April 1521 (standard account; NOT searched again in this session: confirm with one source before the build). The trivia says it |
| s36, s42 | Trade for food between the island and the fleet | Pigafetta records gifts and food exchanged at Mazaua (Wikipedia, First Mass: "Gifts were exchanged by the two parties"). The fish-for-rice rate and Mang Laon's rule are invented |
| s45 to s48 | People beat pots when a great serpent goes for the moon; "Ang Bakunawa! Kakainin niya ang buwan!" | Folklore, recorded: Wikipedia, Bakunawa (Visayan and Bikol; people rushed out "clanging pots and pans to startle the Bakunawa"); Fr Tomás Ortiz, Práctica del Ministerio (1731), as quoted in Zúñiga's Estadismo (1893): at an eclipse people go out and strike things "with great force and violence". The story shows NO eclipse and gives no astronomical event to the night of 31 March 1521: the serpent climbs toward a full moon and leaves it whole |
| Trivia | 31 March 1521; Pigafetta the eyewitness; about fifty men; the two rajahs kissed the cross and knelt; baptisms at Cebu two weeks later; Limasawa per the NHCP (2020), Butuan per others; the Bakunawa of the Visayas and Bikol; the note of 1731 | Rows above |

**Hero lines.** No real person speaks. Magellan, the priest, Pigafetta, Rajah Kolambu and Rajah Siagu are seen
from the trees and say nothing; no attested line of theirs suits the scene, so none is used. The diwa in the
medallion (Rajah Kolambu's, by the series' list of the twelve datu) has ONE invented line under the owner's
exception, "Sa tapat na palitan, walang natatalo." (s75): about trade, nothing outside his own life and time,
and never offered as a quotation. No tender beat: no wife or partner is recorded in the scene.

## 3. Guard rails checked for this script

- **The Mass.** Seen from the trees, far, in two shots that never go nearer than the watchers' own distance.
  From the storyteller's first word of it (s28) to his last (s32) nobody else speaks; the only line before it
  is "Dito lang tayo. Tahimik." There is no joke, no drink in hand and no phone inside it. The priest has no
  words. What is told (fifty men, two rajahs, the cross kissed, the kneeling) is Pigafetta's. Indio's aside
  comes only after "Nang matapos ang misa".
- Nothing mirrors the rite elsewhere: no count of guests, no shared cup, no bread; the golden glass is far
  from it, in the present.
- God has no words; no scripture is used. No church, living person, party or firm has words or a part.
- Foreigners are "mga dayuhang taga-barko" and "panauhin": people, at a distance. The real outcome stands: the
  Mass is said, the fleet stays some days and sails on.
- Usman is only inside the jump. No talk of "kathang-isip" in the story. Trivia at the end. No follow card.
- Marks: none (BIBLE section 2: none from 9 to 19). Knots: still two. The capture fails at the third beat.
