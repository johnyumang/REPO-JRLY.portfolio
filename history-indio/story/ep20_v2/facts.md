## 1. What is real in this script, and where it is said

| Line (sentences.json) | Claim | Status and sources |
|---|---|---|
| s9 (INDIO) | "Lechon: galing sa Espanyol na 'lechón': biik na sumususo pa." | **Well supported, two sources.** Spanish "lechón" is a suckling pig, from "leche", milk (Wikipedia "Lechon" / "Suckling pig"; the dictionary sense of the Spanish word). The same article says the Filipino word has widened to any roasted pig, usually a grown one. The script does not say when the word arrived and does not say the dish came from Spain (roasting pigs is older here; Cebuano "inasal") |
| s10 (INDIO) | Cebu is famous for it ("na sikat diyan, amoy pa lang, ulam na") | **Commonly told, as a reputation, not a measured fact.** GMA News and the Freeman both describe Cebu lechon as the best known, seasoned so that it needs no sauce. "Amoy pa lang, ulam na" is Indio's joke. No shop, brand or living person is named or shown. Claims about when Cebu lechon began (1920s) are inconsistent and are NOT used |
| s7, s17 | Mactan, in Cebu; a marker by the shore | Certain as a place. The Mactan Shrine is in Lapu-Lapu City (Wikipedia "Mactan Shrine"). The marker is read but NOT quoted, and no monument is named or shown as a door |
| s18 | Mactan, before dawn of 27 April 1521 | Certain. Wikipedia "Lapulapu" (they arrived "three hours before dawn"); Wikipedia "Battle of Mactan" |
| s23 to s25 (BUYONG) | The ships cannot come close because of the reef; the men must wade | Certain as Pigafetta's account: rock outcroppings and coral kept the ships and their guns out of range, and the landing party waded in (Wikipedia "Lapulapu"; "Battle of Mactan"). "Hanggang hita" is in the picture note only (Pigafetta: water to the thighs) [summary only] |
| s26 (INDIO) against s34 to s37 | "Sabi sa libro ko, si Lapulapu mismo ang pumatay kay Magellan." | The episode's sabi-sabi. **The sources do not say whose hand struck.** Pigafetta describes many warriors rushing Magellan (Wikipedia "Battle of Mactan"). The story therefore shows a crowd from far and lets Usman say nobody can tell (s36, s37). It does NOT say Lapulapu did not fight, and does not say he did |

Very little else is reliably known (Wikipedia "Lapulapu"). Where he stood, and that he faced his men at dawn, is staging; he is seen from behind and far, with no readable face |
| s28, s29 (LAPULAPU) | "May sibat sila? May sibat din tayo: kawayan, pinatigas sa apoy." | **HERO LINE: A RENDERING IN SUBSTANCE, NOT A QUOTE.** No words of Lapulapu survive. Pigafetta reports only the substance of the answer sent back from Mactan to Magellan's message the night before: that if the Europeans had lances, they had lances of bamboo and stakes hardened with fire. (Found in several transcriptions of the Robertson translation [summary only]; Pigafetta gives it as the reply of the people of Mactan, "they replied".) Invented here: the Tagalog wording, that the chief himself says it, that he says it to his own warriors, and that he says it at dawn on the beach. The second half of the reported answer (the request not to attack at night) is left out. The trivia says plainly that it is a summary of what Pigafetta reported |
| s30 (BUYONG) | "Ayaw niyang yumuko sa hari nila." | Certain in substance: he refused to obey the king of Spain, to accept Humabon as overlord, and to pay tribute (Wikipedia "Lapulapu"; "Battle of Mactan"). Humabon is not named, to keep one thread |
| s31 | The foreigners wade in and are met by a great many warriors | Pigafetta: 49 landed, 11 stayed with the boats; about 1,500 against them (Wikipedia "Battle of Mactan", citing Pigafetta). The numbers are his alone, so they are spoken only in the trivia with "ayon kay Pigafetta" |
| s32, s33 (BUYONG) | Their chests are iron, so the warriors aim at the legs | Pigafetta, as carried by the Inquirer column and Wikipedia "Battle of Mactan" ("unarmored legs"): seeing the armour, the defenders aimed at the legs [summary only; the wording in Robertson was not confirmed] |
| s34 | Magellan, their captain, falls in the shallows | Certain: he was killed in the shallows on 27 April 1521. His wounds (arm, leg) are true and are NOT shown or said: no gore |
| s35 (BUYONG) | The foreigners retreat | Certain: the survivors withdrew to the boats |
| s36, s37 (USMAN) | Nobody can say who struck | See s26. Usman speaks only of what could be smelled and seen from the mangroves |
| s55, trivia | The siyokoy are scaled sea people of folklore; the name is said to come from Hokkien | Folklore: Wikipedia "Siyokoy" (scales, fins, said to drown people; etymology from Hokkien "shui gui", after Manuel 1948). One source for the etymology, so the trivia says "daw" |
| s80 (M) | "Ang Kalasag, kay Lapulapu." | The medallion is invented; Lapulapu is real. Nothing says he owned, made or left any such object |
| Trivia | Magellan killed at Mactan, 27 April 1521; Pigafetta the one detailed witness; 49 against about 1,500 by his count; no record of whose hand; no exact words of Lapulapu survive | As above |

**Hero line:** Lapulapu, s28 to s29, a rendering in substance (see the row). It is not attested as his words and
is not "firmly traditional" as a sentence; it is used because the substance is in the one eyewitness account,
and the trivia does not call it a quote. If the owner wants only attested or traditional lines, cut s28 to s29
and let Buyong say it as "ang sagot namin kagabi". **No tender beat:** no wife or partner of his is known
from the sources and none is shown.

**Not said as fact anywhere:** that Lapulapu killed Magellan with his own hand; any age, face or family of
Lapulapu; the Aginid; the legend that he turned to stone.

## 3. Guard rails checked for this script

- The battle is seen from the mangroves, far, with no blood and no wound shown or said. Magellan and his men
  are far and silent; they are soldiers, not monsters. Lapulapu is seen from behind, no likeness.
- No invented act puts guilt or a false belief on a real person: Lapulapu's one line is the reported answer in
  substance; nothing says who killed Magellan. History stands: Magellan falls, the foreigners withdraw.
- No living person, firm, brand or church has words or a part. Lechon: no shop or person named. "Sabi sa libro
  ko" names nobody. No sacred rite is shown. No scripture.
- Usman is in no present-day scene. Rico and Indio are in the same place, which BIBLE ruling 10 allows at 20.
- The star never leaves the umbrella handle; the map (not Ang Kasulatan) becomes the key.
- No sentence says anything is invented. Trivia at the end. No follow card.
- "He confessed, so it worked" is not used; "it was never the bronze" is not said.

## 4. Could not verify, or weak

- Pigafetta in Robertson's translation was not read in full: the reply about bamboo lances, the aim at the
  legs and the wading rest on transcriptions and summaries. Read volume XXXIII before the build.
- "Hinuhukay na namin ang bahura" (s16): an invented wrong by an invented man; check that no real reclamation
  or dredging dispute at Mactan is being mirrored before the build.
- The names Buyong, Tobías Alcaín and Rico Bulan against living people.
- The present-day part before the jump (s6 to s17) is about 80 words, about 35 seconds.
